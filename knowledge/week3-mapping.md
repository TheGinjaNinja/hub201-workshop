# Week 3 Content Mapping

## Overview

Week 3 covers Customer Discovery and ICP (Ideal Customer Profile). The frameworks and deliverables below map to a real discovery toolkit built for a pre-seed B2B startup. They show what a startup can produce when they follow the methodology with the right inputs.

## Deliverable Mapping

| Deliverable | Week 3 Topic | What It Teaches |
|---|---|---|
| Validation Canvas | Assumptions prioritisation, three fits framework | How to map Customer/Problem Fit, Problem/Solution Fit, and Revenue Model Fit in a single view. Forces founders to identify which fit they are testing and what evidence they need. |
| Assumptions Map | PULL hypotheses, experiment design | How to turn assumptions into testable hypotheses using the PULL format: "We believe [segment] will [action] because [reason], and we will know we are right when [outcome]." Includes experiment cards with success criteria. |
| Discovery Questions | Customer interview scripting | How to write discovery questions that test specific assumptions without leading the interviewee. Maps each question to the assumption it validates. Includes ground rules (no pitching, no leading, behaviour over opinion). |
| Methodology Guide | Discovery cadence, Say-Do Gap, evidence ladder | How to run a 3-month discovery sprint: weekly interview cadence, evidence grading (strong/moderate/weak), the Say-Do Gap principle (what people say they will do vs what they actually do), and the Concierge MVP approach. |
| Pilot Offer | Commitment testing, Concierge MVP | How to structure a low-friction pilot that tests willingness to pay, not just willingness to talk. Performance-based pricing as a commitment test. The pilot itself is the experiment. |
| Savings One-Pager | Sales collateral from discovery evidence | How to turn discovery insights into a customer-facing document that quantifies the opportunity. Leads with the customer's numbers, not the startup's features. |

## Key Frameworks for Week 3

### Say-Do Gap
What customers say they will do and what they actually do are different things. Discovery must test behaviour, not collect opinions. "Would you use this?" is worthless. "Show me how you solve this today" reveals truth.

### PULL Hypothesis
Structured format for testable assumptions:
- **P**opulation: Who are we testing with?
- **U**nderstanding: What do we believe about them?
- **L**earning: What experiment will we run?
- **L**imit: What is our success threshold?

### Evidence Ladder
- **Strong:** Observed behaviour, payments, repeat usage, measurable workflow change
- **Moderate:** Stated commitments, signed LOIs, pilot agreements with dates
- **Weak:** Opinions, expressed interest, "I would definitely use that"
- **Assumption:** Untested belief, logic-based reasoning with no market data

### Concierge MVP
Do the work manually for the first customers before building the product. This tests whether the outcome is valuable, not whether the technology works. If customers will not accept the outcome delivered by hand, they will not accept it delivered by software.
