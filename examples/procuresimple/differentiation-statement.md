# Differentiation Statement: ProcureSimple

## What We Do Differently
We give SMEs (50-500 employees) enterprise-grade procurement visibility and collective buying power on a performance-based pricing model where customers pay nothing unless we deliver measurable savings. Existing procurement tools are built for enterprise buyers with clean ERP data and six-figure budgets. Manual brokers exist but offer no analytics, no spend visibility, and no scalable pricing model that aligns their incentives with the customer's.

## Evidence for Differentiation
- 6 discovery interviews with operations and finance leads at SMEs confirmed that procurement is entirely manual and opaque. All 6 described spreadsheet-based or no-process-at-all approaches to supplier management.
- 5 of 6 interviewees said they would not pay upfront for procurement software but would share savings if the tool delivered them. This directly validates the performance-based pricing model over traditional per-seat SaaS.
- 4 of 6 had no idea what they spent with individual suppliers across categories. They could not answer "how much did you spend on office supplies last year?" without digging through invoices manually.
- Competitor review of SpendMaster (enterprise, recently acquired), ProcurePlus (ERP module requiring clean data), and TradeLink (manual broker, no platform) confirmed that no existing solution targets SMEs with a performance-based model.

**Honest note on "collective buying power":** The thesis is that aggregating demand across many SME customers creates buying power that no single SME could achieve alone. This is the long-term flywheel. However, with 6 interviews and zero customers, the collective element is entirely aspirational. A single customer gets spend visibility and negotiation support from the ProcureSimple team. The collective advantage only kicks in when there is meaningful volume across multiple customers in overlapping supplier categories. If the collective buying power claim were removed entirely, the product still offers value through spend analytics on messy invoice data and a risk-free pricing model. The network effect is the strategic bet, not today's reality. Do not lead with it in early sales conversations.

## Defensibility
- **Type:** Network effects (building toward, not established)
- **Current strength:** Theoretical. No customers, no aggregated demand, no supplier relationships. The performance-based pricing model is a commercial innovation but is not hard to copy.
- **Building toward:** More customers means more buying power, which means better supplier rates, which means higher savings delivered, which means more customers. This is a classic network effect flywheel. Over the next 12-18 months, ProcureSimple aims to build enough customer density in 2-3 supplier categories to demonstrate that collective negotiation delivers meaningfully better rates than any single SME could achieve alone. This is entirely unproven at pre-seed.

**Defensibility reality check:** At pre-seed, ProcureSimple has no defensibility. The performance-based pricing model is easy to replicate. The spend analytics on messy data is a genuine technical challenge but not a moat. The only real long-term defensibility is customer density in specific supplier categories, and that does not exist yet. An enterprise procurement vendor with existing supplier relationships could build a downmarket SME offering faster than ProcureSimple can build up from zero. The honest assessment is that execution speed and customer acquisition in a market that incumbents have ignored are the only advantages today.

## Path
- **Path:** Adjacency
- **Rationale:** ProcureSimple is adjacent to existing procurement platforms on two dimensions: customer segment (SMEs with 50-500 employees, not enterprise) and business model (performance-based pricing, not per-seat SaaS or enterprise licensing). SpendMaster could theoretically move downmarket, but it would require rebuilding for messy SME data, restructuring pricing, hiring a different sales team, and accepting fundamentally different unit economics. ProcurePlus is an ERP module that requires clean data SMEs do not have. TradeLink is a manual broker with no platform. Cost and customer overlap with any existing solution is well below 90%, which confirms adjacency rather than direct competition.

## Full Differentiation Statement (MVP Format)
For Operations Directors and Finance Managers at SMEs (50-500 employees) who waste time on manual procurement and have no visibility into what they spend with suppliers,
ProcureSimple is a spend analytics and procurement platform that delivers measurable savings on a pay-for-performance basis.
Unlike SpendMaster and ProcurePlus, we work from messy invoice data rather than clean ERP exports, price on a share of realised savings rather than per-seat licensing, and are built specifically for SMEs who cannot justify enterprise procurement tools.

## Next Steps
1. Run a concierge MVP with 2-3 SME customers. Manually ingest their invoice data, produce a spend analysis, and negotiate one supplier contract on their behalf. The differentiation statement claims "measurable savings" but currently has none to show. This is the biggest gap.
2. Validate the performance-based pricing model with real numbers. What percentage of savings is fair? 5 of 6 interviewees said they would share savings, but nobody has agreed to a specific number. Test pricing at 15%, 20%, and 25% of realised savings to find the threshold.
3. Test the messy data claim. Can you actually build useful spend analytics from invoices, bank statements, and email receipts without clean ERP data? Build the ingestion pipeline for 2-3 real SME datasets before claiming this works.
4. Define the supplier category strategy for collective buying power. Which 2-3 supplier categories have enough overlap across SME customers to demonstrate collective negotiation value? This cannot be "all categories." Pick a beachhead and prove the model there first.
5. Interview 10-15 more SMEs to strengthen the evidence base. 6 interviews is a start, not validation. Push for broader coverage across industries and company sizes within the 50-500 range.
