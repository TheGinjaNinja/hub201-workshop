## Competitor: SpendMaster

### Overview
SpendMaster is an enterprise procurement platform, originally built for mid-market companies but repositioned toward enterprise after being acquired by a large software group. Strong spend analytics, deep ERP integrations across SAP, Oracle, and NetSuite. Approximately 60+ employees. Their customer base is primarily companies with 1,000+ employees and mature procurement functions with dedicated teams and clean data.

### Their Positioning
"Enterprise procurement intelligence platform." They lead with analytics depth, spend visibility, and integration breadth. Their marketing emphasises dashboards, category management, and strategic procurement workflows designed for procurement teams of 5-20 people.

### Where They Win
- Analytics depth. Their spend analytics are genuinely best-in-class for companies that have clean, structured procurement data flowing from ERP systems.
- Brand recognition. Procurement leaders at larger companies know the name. Analysts cover them. The acquisition by a major software group added credibility.
- Established customer base. Hundreds of enterprise customers providing case studies and reference calls.
- Integration breadth. Native integrations with every major ERP, P2P, and accounting system.
- Enterprise sales infrastructure. Dedicated sales engineers, implementation consultants, and customer success managers. ProcureSimple has two founders and a product prototype.

### Where We Win
| Our Advantage | Their Limitation | Customer Outcome |
|---------------|-----------------|------------------|
| Performance-based pricing. We take a percentage of savings delivered. | Per-seat pricing at EUR 2-5K/month regardless of whether the customer saves anything. For a 200-person company, that is EUR 24-60K/year before implementation costs. | The SME pays nothing unless ProcureSimple delivers measurable savings. Zero risk to try. SpendMaster charges whether or not they deliver value. |
| Works with messy data. Invoices, spreadsheets, email threads. No clean ERP feed required. | Built for structured data from enterprise ERP systems. If the customer does not have clean procurement data, SpendMaster's analytics produce garbage. | The 200-person company that manages procurement through spreadsheets and email can start immediately. No data cleanup project required before getting value. |
| Onboarding in days, not months. Lightweight setup, no ERP integration required to start. | Enterprise implementation cycles of 3-6 months. Requires IT involvement, ERP configuration, data migration, and training. | The ops manager who is already drowning in procurement admin does not have to run a 6-month implementation project on top of their day job. They can see results within the first week. |
| Collective buying power. Aggregates spend across multiple SMEs to negotiate better supplier rates. | Serves each customer individually. No cross-customer aggregation or collective negotiation leverage. | A 200-person company gets the supplier pricing that normally requires the purchasing volume of a 2,000-person company. Individual SMEs could never negotiate these rates alone. |

### Common Objections and Responses
| Objection | Response | Evidence |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| "SpendMaster has better analytics." | "They do. The question is whether you need enterprise-grade analytics or whether you need savings. SpendMaster charges EUR 2-5K/month whether you save anything or not. We charge a percentage of savings we actually deliver. If we do not save you money, you pay nothing. Which model makes more sense for a company your size?" | Weak (pricing comparison is from desk research. ProcureSimple has not competed against SpendMaster in a real deal.) |
| "SpendMaster is an established vendor. You are a startup." | "Fair point. Two things to consider. First, SpendMaster was built for companies 5-10x your size. Their product assumes you have a procurement team, clean ERP data, and a 6-month implementation budget. You have an ops manager with a spreadsheet. Second, our model means you only pay when we deliver. If we underperform, you stop. There is no 12-month enterprise contract locking you in." | Weak (the implementation timeline and contract structure claims are from public marketing materials, not from customers who have evaluated both.) |
| "We should wait until we are bigger and then use SpendMaster." | "By the time you are big enough for SpendMaster, you will have overpaid suppliers by 10-15% for years. That is money that compounds. A company spending EUR 2M/year on procurement that saves even 5% is recovering EUR 100K annually. Every year you wait is another year of that leakage." | Assumption (the 10-15% savings figure is an industry benchmark, not validated with ProcureSimple's own customers.) |

### Killer Questions
1. "How long did SpendMaster's implementation take for your last client of this size? And what did the implementation cost on top of the subscription?"
2. "What percentage of your procurement spend can you actually categorise and analyse today? If the answer is less than 80%, SpendMaster's analytics will have blind spots from day one."
3. "Does SpendMaster's pricing model change if the platform does not deliver measurable savings in the first year? Or do you pay the same regardless?"

### Landmines
1. "It is worth asking any procurement vendor how their platform handles unstructured data. If your procurement runs through invoices, spreadsheets, and email rather than a clean ERP feed, some platforms will require a data cleanup project before you see any value. That project alone can cost more than the platform subscription."
2. "One thing to explore: what is the minimum company size where the vendor has successful deployments? Some platforms work brilliantly at 1,000+ employees but have never successfully served a 200-person company. The workflows, data quality, and team structures are fundamentally different."

## Competitor: Do Nothing / Spreadsheets (Status Quo)

### Overview
The prospect continues managing procurement through spreadsheets, email, and personal supplier relationships. The ops manager or finance lead handles procurement as one of many responsibilities. There is no dedicated procurement function, no spend visibility, and no systematic supplier management. This is ProcureSimple's primary competitor. At pre-seed, far more deals will be lost to inaction than to SpendMaster.

### Why They Choose Inaction
- **"Our suppliers are good enough."** They have worked with the same suppliers for years. The relationships feel solid. There is no obvious reason to change.
- **"We do not have time to implement anything new."** The ops manager is already stretched. Adding a new tool feels like more work, not less.
- **"Procurement is not broken, it is just how things are."** Overpaying by 10-15% is invisible when you have never benchmarked. You cannot feel pain you have never measured.
- **"We have always done it this way."** The current process works well enough. Nobody is complaining. There is no crisis forcing action.
- **"We are too small to need procurement software."** The assumption that procurement tools are for big companies with dedicated teams.

### Cost of Inaction
| Current Approach | Time Cost | Money Cost | Risk Cost |
|-----------------|-----------|------------|-----------|
| Ops manager handles procurement manually | 2+ days per month on procurement admin: comparing quotes, chasing suppliers, reconciling invoices, managing renewals. | EUR 2-4K/month in salary time spent on manual procurement work that could be automated. | Key person dependency. If the ops manager leaves, all supplier relationships and institutional knowledge walk out the door. No documentation, no system, no handover. |
| Supplier selection based on habit | Zero time invested in benchmarking. Renewals happen on autopilot. | 10-15% overspend on addressable procurement categories (industry benchmark). For a company spending EUR 1-3M/year on procurement, that is EUR 100-450K in annual savings left on the table. | Supplier concentration risk. No visibility into whether you are over-reliant on single suppliers. No leverage in negotiations because you have no market data. |
| No spend visibility | No time spent on analysis because there is nothing to analyse. | Hidden costs: duplicate subscriptions, missed volume discounts, inconsistent pricing across departments. Typical finding in first procurement audit: 5-8% of spend is waste or duplication. | Cannot answer basic questions from the board or investors about procurement efficiency. As the company grows past 100 employees, this becomes a governance gap. |

### Objections and Responses
| Objection | Response | Evidence |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| "We do not have time to implement anything new." | "Understood. Your ops manager is spending 2+ days a month on procurement admin. That is the time cost of not having a system. We onboard in days, not months. After the first week, that 2 days starts shrinking. The question is whether you can afford to keep spending senior staff time on manual supplier management." | Moderate (the 2+ days/month figure came from 4 of 6 discovery interviews. The onboarding timeline is a target, not yet proven in production.) |
| "Our suppliers are good enough." | "They might be. When was the last time you benchmarked your top 5 supplier rates against the market? Most companies we speak to have not done this in over a year. In that time, prices shift, new suppliers enter the market, and volume discounts change. 'Good enough' is an assumption unless you have tested it recently." | Moderate (3 interviewees confirmed they had not benchmarked suppliers in 12+ months. The implication that prices have shifted is reasonable but not validated with specific examples.) |
| "We are too small for procurement software." | "You are too small for enterprise procurement software. That is different. SpendMaster needs clean ERP data and a 6-month implementation. We work with your invoices and spreadsheets. If you are spending more than EUR 500K/year on suppliers, there are savings to find. And our model means you only pay if we find them." | Weak (the EUR 500K threshold is an assumption about minimum viable spend for the platform to deliver value. Not yet validated.) |
| "Procurement is not a priority right now." | "I get that. Can I ask one question: what percentage of your total revenue goes to procurement? For most manufacturing SMEs it is 40-60%. If you could reduce that by 5%, what does that do to your margins? Procurement is not glamorous, but it is often the single biggest lever for improving profitability that nobody is pulling." | Moderate (the 40-60% revenue figure is a standard manufacturing SME benchmark. The 5% reduction is conservative but not validated with ProcureSimple's own data.) |

### Questions That Create Urgency
1. "What percentage of your total revenue goes to procurement? If you could reduce that by even 5%, what would that mean for your margins this year?"
2. "When was the last time you benchmarked your top 5 supplier rates against the market? If you have not done it in the last 6 months, how do you know you are not overpaying?"
3. "If your ops manager left tomorrow, how would you reconstruct your supplier relationships, pricing agreements, and renewal dates? Is any of that documented anywhere?"
4. "How many hours per month does your team spend on procurement admin: getting quotes, chasing invoices, comparing suppliers? What would you do with that time back?"

## Evidence Assessment

- **Overall rating:** Assumption-Heavy
- **Strongest evidence:** The "Do Nothing" battlecard is grounded in actual discovery conversations. 4 of 6 interviewees described spending 2+ days per month on manual procurement admin. 3 confirmed they had not benchmarked suppliers in over a year. These are real data points from real conversations.
- **Biggest gap:** ProcureSimple has never competed against SpendMaster in a real sales process. The entire SpendMaster battlecard is constructed from desk research, public pricing pages, and marketing materials. Every "where we win" claim and every objection response is untested theory. Until ProcureSimple encounters SpendMaster in an actual deal, this battlecard is speculation.
- **Competitor knowledge depth:** Weak for SpendMaster (website research and public materials only. No demo seen, no SpendMaster customers interviewed, no competitive deal experience). Moderate for Do Nothing (based on 6 discovery interviews where the current state of procurement was discussed in detail).
- **Key assumption to validate:** The collective buying power claim is ProcureSimple's most distinctive positioning, but it requires a critical mass of SME customers to deliver. At pre-seed with zero customers, this is a promise, not a capability. If the aggregation model does not produce materially better supplier rates, the core differentiator disappears.

## Next Steps

1. Request a SpendMaster demo. Go through their sales process as a buyer. Understand their pitch, their onboarding flow, their actual pricing for a 200-person company, and how they handle prospects without clean ERP data. Do not compete against a product you have never seen.
2. Find 2-3 companies that evaluated SpendMaster but did not buy. Understand why they walked away. These conversations will reveal whether ProcureSimple's assumed advantages match the real reasons SMEs reject enterprise procurement tools.
3. Validate the collective buying power model. Talk to 5+ potential SME customers and ask: "If we could aggregate your purchasing volume with other similar companies to negotiate better supplier rates, would that change how you think about procurement?" Test whether this concept resonates before building it into every sales conversation.
4. Document the actual procurement spend breakdown at your 6 interview companies. Get specific numbers: what do they spend annually on procurement, in which categories, with how many suppliers? Real data replaces the "EUR 1-3M/year" industry estimate with specific, credible figures.
5. After the first pilot customer converts (or does not), document every objection, every stakeholder involved, and every decision point. This real sales data replaces the assumptions in this battlecard with evidence from an actual buying process.
