# Value Proposition: ProcureSimple

## Target Customer
- **Persona:** "Spreadsheet-Drowning Karel" (Operations Manager at a mid-market European manufacturing SME)
- **Profile:** Operations Manager or Head of Procurement at a manufacturing SME with 50-500 employees (beachhead: 50-200), based in Europe, managing EUR 5-20M in annual supplier spend across fragmented categories
- **Trigger event:** Rising input costs squeezing margins, with board pressure to find measurable cost savings without hiring a full procurement team
- **Evidence source:** 6 discovery interviews with operations managers at manufacturing and retail SMEs in Europe. All 6 described procurement as manual, fragmented, and lacking visibility. (Moderate)

## Customer Jobs (ranked by importance)
1. Find ways to reduce procurement costs so the business can protect margins as input prices rise - Evidence: 6 of 6 interviewees described cost pressure as a top priority from the board (Moderate)
2. Get visibility into total supplier spend across the business so they can identify where money is going - Evidence: 5 of 6 interviewees said they cannot produce a reliable total spend figure without days of manual work (Moderate)
3. Run procurement processes (RFQs, supplier selection, contract renewals) without it consuming all their time - Evidence: 4 of 6 interviewees described procurement admin as a major time drain on top of their other responsibilities (Moderate)

## Customer Pains (ranked by severity)
1. No consolidated view of supplier spend. Data is scattered across spreadsheets, email inboxes, and finance systems, making it impossible to see total spend by category or supplier without manual aggregation. - Severity: Critical - Evidence: 5 of 6 interviewees confirmed they cannot produce a spend breakdown without significant manual effort (Moderate)
2. No negotiating leverage. Each company negotiates individually with suppliers, paying higher unit prices than larger competitors. They know they are overpaying but have no mechanism to change it. - Severity: Critical - Evidence: 6 of 6 interviewees acknowledged they lack leverage. 4 specifically mentioned losing out to larger buyers. (Moderate)
3. RFQ and supplier comparison processes are entirely manual. Operations managers spend hours emailing suppliers, collecting quotes in spreadsheets, and comparing line items by hand. - Severity: Major - Evidence: 4 of 6 interviewees walked through their current process, all involving email chains and spreadsheets (Moderate)
4. No way to benchmark whether current supplier pricing is competitive. Without market data or peer comparisons, operations managers cannot tell whether they are getting a fair deal. - Severity: Minor - Evidence: 2 interviewees mentioned this. Others had not considered benchmarking as something accessible to them. (Weak)

## Customer Gains (ranked by relevance)
1. Reduce procurement costs by 10-15% on key spend categories through collective buying power - Relevance: Essential - Evidence: All 6 interviewees confirmed that a 10%+ saving would be significant enough to justify adopting a new tool. 3 said even 5% would get board attention. (Moderate)
2. Get real-time visibility into total supplier spend, broken down by category, supplier, and time period - Relevance: Essential - Evidence: 5 of 6 interviewees said spend visibility is a prerequisite for any cost reduction initiative (Moderate)
3. Free up operations manager time currently spent on procurement admin so they can focus on production and logistics - Relevance: Nice-to-have - Evidence: 3 interviewees mentioned time savings as a secondary benefit. Most cared more about cost savings than efficiency. (Weak)
4. Build a more professional, data-driven procurement function without hiring a dedicated procurement team - Relevance: Nice-to-have - Evidence: Assumption based on the profile of companies too small for a procurement hire but too large to ignore procurement. No interviewee used this framing. (Assumption)

## Pain Relievers (mapped to pains)
| Pain | Pain Reliever | How it works |
|------|--------------|--------------|
| No consolidated spend visibility | Spend analytics dashboard | Imports data from invoices, ERP exports, and spreadsheets. Categorises spend automatically and surfaces a live breakdown by supplier, category, and period. |
| No negotiating leverage as individual buyers | Collective buying groups | Aggregates demand across multiple SMEs in the same spend category. Negotiates volume-based rates with suppliers on behalf of the group. Each company benefits from the combined volume. |
| Manual RFQ and supplier comparison | Streamlined RFQ workflow | Standardised RFQ templates, supplier response collection in one place, and side-by-side comparison views. Replaces email chains with a structured process. |
| Cannot benchmark supplier pricing | Category benchmarking data | Shows anonymised pricing data from other buyers in the same category, so operations managers can see whether their current rates are above or below the group average. |

## Gain Creators (mapped to gains)
| Gain | Gain Creator | How it works |
|------|-------------|--------------|
| Reduce procurement costs by 10-15% | Collective purchasing engine | Groups SMEs by spend category (e.g. packaging, raw materials, logistics). Negotiates consolidated contracts with preferred suppliers. Savings are split between buyers, with ProcureSimple taking a percentage of realised savings only. |
| Real-time spend visibility | Automated spend categorisation | Ingests invoice and purchase order data, applies category tagging, and presents a live dashboard. No manual spreadsheet consolidation required. |
| Free up operations manager time | Managed RFQ process | ProcureSimple handles supplier outreach, quote collection, and comparison for selected categories. The operations manager reviews a shortlist rather than managing the entire process. |

## ROI Ladder
- **Feature:** Collective buying groups that aggregate demand across multiple SMEs in the same spend category
- **Capability:** SMEs can access volume-based pricing normally reserved for larger buyers, without coordinating directly with other companies
- **Benefit:** Operations managers achieve meaningful cost reductions on their largest spend categories without lengthy individual negotiations
- **Outcome:** Procurement costs drop by 10-15% on addressable categories within the first 6 months, with full transparency into what was saved and where
- **ROI:** On EUR 5M addressable spend, a 10-15% saving produces EUR 500-750K in annual cost reduction. ProcureSimple charges roughly 20% of realised savings, so the customer keeps EUR 400-600K. Payback is immediate because the fee is performance-based.

## Ad Lib Value Proposition Statement

> Our collective procurement platform helps operations managers at European manufacturing SMEs with 50-500 employees who need to reduce supplier costs and get spend visibility by aggregating buying power across multiple companies and replacing manual spreadsheet-based processes with automated spend analytics, unlike traditional purchasing consultants who charge fixed retainers regardless of results and provide one-off recommendations rather than ongoing savings.

## Evidence Assessment
- **Overall rating:** Moderate
- **Strongest evidence:** The spend visibility pain. Five of six interviewees independently described the same problem: they cannot produce a reliable spend breakdown without days of manual work. This was the most consistent signal across all interviews. The cost pressure trigger is also strong, with all six confirming board-level urgency around margins.
- **Biggest assumption:** The 10-15% savings claim through collective buying power. This is the core value proposition, but it has not been validated with actual group negotiations yet. No pilot has been run, no supplier has been approached with aggregated volume, and no savings have been realised. If the collective model does not produce meaningfully better rates than individual negotiation, the entire platform thesis weakens to a spend analytics tool. The performance-based revenue model also depends on savings being clearly measurable and attributable, which has not been tested in practice.

## Next Steps
1. Run a pilot with 3-5 SMEs in the same spend category (e.g. packaging or logistics) in one European market. Negotiate a collective rate with at least one supplier and measure the actual savings versus each company's existing rates. This is the single most important evidence gap. Without pilot data, the 10-15% savings claim is an assumption.
2. Validate willingness to share spend data. The collective model requires companies to share at least category-level spend information with the platform. Two interviewees expressed hesitation about sharing supplier pricing with a third party. Test whether anonymisation and aggregation are sufficient to overcome this, or whether data sharing is a blocker.
3. Test the revenue model with real numbers. The 20% of realised savings model sounds attractive, but it requires clear attribution. Interview 2-3 finance managers or CFOs at target companies to understand whether they would accept ProcureSimple's savings calculations, or whether disputes over "what counts as a saving" would create friction.
4. Clarify the competitive landscape. Two interviewees mentioned occasionally using purchasing consultants. Map out how many of the target companies have tried formal procurement help before, what they paid, and why they stopped. This will sharpen the positioning against the current alternative.
