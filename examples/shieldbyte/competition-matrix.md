# Competition Matrix: ShieldByte

## 1. Competitive Alternatives

**Direct competitors:**
- KnowBe4 (US, dominant global player in security awareness training)
- Cofense (US, enterprise-focused phishing simulation and response)
- Hoxhunt (Finland, gamified security awareness with adaptive elements)

**Adjacent products:**
- Proofpoint Security Awareness Training (part of a broader email security suite, not standalone)

**Manual processes / consultants:**
- Local IT consultancies in Serbia offering manual phishing tests and in-person training workshops
- Internal IT teams running ad-hoc phishing tests using free tools (GoPhish, manual email campaigns)

**Do nothing:**
- Accept the risk. Many mid-market banks in the region currently have no formal security awareness programme. They rely on a brief onboarding presentation and hope for the best.

## 4. Qualifiers vs. Differentiators

**Qualifiers (table stakes):**
- Phishing simulation templates: Every player in this space has them. ShieldByte needs a minimum viable library to be taken seriously, but templates alone will not win deals.
- Micro-training on failure: KnowBe4 and Hoxhunt both offer this. It is expected, not distinctive.
- Ease of deployment: Customers expect reasonable time-to-value. Not a selling point unless competitors are notably bad at it.

**Differentiators (ShieldByte's edge):**
- Serbian/Balkan language content: No global vendor offers this. Local consultancies have it but lack a platform. This is the clearest differentiator for the beachhead market.
- NIS2 compliance reporting tailored to Southeast European regulatory context: Global vendors offer generic compliance reports. ShieldByte can map specifically to the regional implementation of NIS2.
- Pricing accessible to mid-market banks (200-1000 employees): Global vendors price for enterprise. ShieldByte can undercut significantly and still build a viable business at regional scale.

**Irrelevant (for now):**
- Advanced SIEM integration: Mid-market Serbian banks mostly do not have sophisticated SIEM deployments. Deep integration is not a buying criterion for the beachhead. It will matter later for larger customers.

**Aspirations:**
- Behaviour change measurement over time: Hoxhunt does this well. ShieldByte's prototype tracks it but has no real data yet. If 90-day pilots produce strong before/after data, this moves from aspiration to differentiator.
- Adaptive difficulty as a proven differentiator: The engine exists in prototype, but there is no evidence yet that adaptive simulations produce better outcomes than static ones. Pilot data will determine whether this is real or marketing.

## 5. Positioning Opportunity Statement

**Path: Adjacency**

ShieldByte is positioned as an adjacent player to global security awareness platforms. The adjacency is defined by two dimensions: geography (Southeast Europe, with local language and regulatory context) and customer segment (mid-market banks with 200-1000 employees, below the typical enterprise threshold of global vendors).

KnowBe4 and Cofense could theoretically serve this market, but doing so would require building Serbian language content, mapping NIS2 compliance for the region, and creating pricing models that work for banks one-tenth the size of their typical customer. This is not a single feature gap. It is a fundamentally different market that requires different content, different pricing, and different support.

## 7. Next Steps

1. Validate the beachhead market size. Build a named target list of Serbian banks with 200-1000 employees. Confirm the actual count. If it is fewer than 30, the beachhead may be too small and you need to include Croatia, Bosnia, and North Macedonia from day one.
2. Run a pricing sensitivity test with 3-5 interviewees. Show them three price points (EUR 8, 12, and 18 per user per year) and gauge reactions. "Cannot afford enterprise pricing" is not the same as "will pay EUR 12/user/year."
3. Complete the two 90-day pilots and publish before/after click rate data. This converts the adaptive difficulty claim from Assumption to Moderate or Strong.
4. Build 2-3 SIEM integrations (even basic ones) to close the table stakes gap. Without any integration capability, enterprise evaluators will disqualify ShieldByte before seeing the differentiators.
5. Talk to 2-3 banks that use KnowBe4 today. Understand their actual experience, not your assumptions about their limitations. If they are happy with KnowBe4, your positioning needs adjusting.
