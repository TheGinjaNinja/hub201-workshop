# Differentiation Statement: ShieldByte

## What We Do Differently
We deliver adaptive phishing simulations and security awareness training in Serbian and Balkan languages, mapped to NIS2 compliance requirements, at a price point built for mid-market banks with 200-1000 employees. Global vendors serve enterprise customers in English. Local consultancies offer training but have no platform, no automation, and no way to measure behaviour change over time.

## Evidence for Differentiation
- 8 discovery interviews with IT security managers at Serbian banks confirmed that language and regulatory relevance are buying criteria, not nice-to-haves. Four interviewees specifically said English-only training reduces employee engagement.
- Website review of KnowBe4, Cofense, Proofpoint, and Hoxhunt confirmed no Serbian language content is available from any global vendor.
- 5 interviewees described budget constraints that would make enterprise pricing (USD 20-30/user/year) difficult to justify internally.
- 2 pilot commitments from Serbian banks suggest the value proposition resonates enough to secure time commitment, though neither has committed money yet.

**Honest note on "AI-powered":** The adaptive difficulty engine uses machine learning to adjust simulation difficulty based on employee performance. However, "AI-powered" alone is not a differentiator. Every security awareness vendor in 2026 claims AI. The differentiator is not the AI. It is what the AI is applied to (Serbian-language phishing patterns targeting Balkan banking employees) and where it is deployed (a market global vendors have ignored). If the adaptive engine were removed and replaced with manually curated difficulty levels, the regional and language differentiation would still hold. The AI is a capability enhancer, not the core differentiator.

## Defensibility
- **Type:** Proprietary data (building toward) + Regulatory/compliance advantage (early signs)
- **Current strength:** Early signs. Serbian language phishing templates and NIS2-mapped reporting are being built during the pilot phase. No competitor has equivalent content today.
- **Building toward:** Over the next 18 months, ShieldByte aims to build a proprietary dataset of phishing simulation response patterns from Southeast European bank employees. This data, once collected across multiple customers, will make the adaptive engine increasingly accurate for this specific market. Additionally, becoming the recognised vendor for NIS2 compliance in the region creates a regulatory positioning advantage that takes time to replicate. Neither of these is established yet. Both are plausible but unproven.

**Defensibility reality check:** The regional language content is a moderate barrier. A well-funded competitor could hire Serbian translators and build equivalent content in 3-6 months. The real defensibility comes from combining language content with local regulatory mapping, local customer relationships, and a growing dataset of regional employee behaviour patterns. No single element is unbreachable. The combination creates the moat.

## Path
- **Path:** Adjacency
- **Rationale:** ShieldByte is adjacent to global security awareness platforms on two dimensions: geography (Southeast Europe) and customer segment (mid-market). KnowBe4 could theoretically enter this market, but it would require building local language content, adapting pricing, hiring regional support staff, and mapping local regulatory requirements. That represents a new market entry for them, not a feature addition. Cost and customer overlap with KnowBe4 is well below 90%, which confirms adjacency rather than direct competition.

## Full Differentiation Statement (MVP Format)
For CISOs and IT Security Managers at mid-market Serbian banks (200-1000 employees) who need to implement NIS2-compliant security awareness programmes,
ShieldByte is an adaptive phishing simulation platform that reduces phishing susceptibility with measurable, auditable results.
Unlike KnowBe4, we deliver Serbian-language simulations with locally relevant phishing scenarios, NIS2-mapped compliance reporting, and pricing built for mid-market banks rather than global enterprise.

## Next Steps
1. Complete the two 90-day pilots and collect quantitative data on phishing click rate reduction. The differentiation statement claims "measurable results" but currently has no results to show. This is the biggest gap.
2. Validate NIS2 compliance reporting with an actual auditor or compliance officer. The claim that reports are "NIS2-mapped" needs external confirmation that the mapping meets audit requirements.
3. Test pricing with 5 more banks. The "pricing built for mid-market" claim is based on interviews where people said enterprise pricing is too expensive. That is not the same as validating that ShieldByte's pricing is right. Run a Van Westendorp or similar pricing exercise.
4. Start building the proprietary dataset strategy. Define what data will be collected during pilots, how it will be anonymised, and how it feeds back into the adaptive engine. This is the long-term defensibility play and it needs to be designed now, not after the pilots end.
