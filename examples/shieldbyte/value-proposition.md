# Value Proposition: ShieldByte

## Target Customer
- **Persona:** "Compliance-First Dragan" (CISO at a mid-market Serbian bank)
- **Profile:** CISO or IT Security Manager at a Serbian bank with 200-1000 employees, regulated under NIS2
- **Trigger event:** NIS2 Directive compliance deadline requiring a documented security awareness programme
- **Evidence source:** 8 discovery interviews with IT security managers at Serbian banks. All 8 mentioned NIS2 as a near-term pressure. (Moderate)

## Customer Jobs (ranked by importance)
1. Demonstrate regulatory compliance with NIS2 security awareness requirements to auditors and the board - Evidence: 8 interviews, all mentioned this unprompted (Moderate)
2. Reduce the organisation's exposure to phishing attacks, which account for the majority of security incidents - Evidence: 6 of 8 interviewees cited phishing as their top incident type (Moderate)
3. Show the board that security investment is producing measurable results, not just ticking boxes - Evidence: 3 interviewees mentioned board pressure for metrics (Weak)

## Customer Pains (ranked by severity)
1. No structured security awareness programme in place, and the NIS2 deadline is approaching - Severity: Critical - Evidence: 7 of 8 interviewees confirmed they have no formal programme (Moderate)
2. Running phishing simulations manually is time-consuming and inconsistent. IT security staff spend hours configuring test emails and tracking who clicked. - Severity: Major - Evidence: 5 interviewees described manual processes using generic email tools (Moderate)
3. Existing training content is in English, which reduces engagement among non-technical staff who work primarily in Serbian - Severity: Major - Evidence: 4 interviewees mentioned language as a barrier (Moderate)
4. Cannot measure whether training is actually changing employee behaviour or just generating completion certificates - Severity: Minor - Evidence: 2 interviewees raised this. Others had not thought about it. (Weak)

## Customer Gains (ranked by relevance)
1. Pass NIS2 compliance audit for security awareness with documented evidence - Relevance: Essential - Evidence: All 8 interviewees confirmed this is a must-have (Moderate)
2. Reduce phishing click rates across the organisation with measurable before/after data - Relevance: Essential - Evidence: 6 interviewees said they need to report metrics to the board (Moderate)
3. Free up IT security staff time currently spent on manual simulation campaigns - Relevance: Nice-to-have - Evidence: 3 interviewees mentioned time savings as a secondary benefit (Weak)
4. Build a security-aware culture where employees recognise threats without IT intervention - Relevance: Nice-to-have - Evidence: Assumption based on industry best practice. No interviewee used this language. (Assumption)

## Pain Relievers (mapped to pains)
| Pain | Pain Reliever | How it works |
|------|--------------|--------------|
| No structured programme for NIS2 | Pre-built compliance-ready awareness programme | Out-of-the-box campaign templates mapped to NIS2 Article 21 requirements, with audit-ready reporting |
| Manual phishing simulations are slow | Automated adaptive phishing campaigns | Platform schedules, sends, and tracks simulations automatically. No manual email configuration needed. |
| Training content is in English only | Serbian and regional language content | Simulations and micro-training modules delivered in Serbian, with Balkan regional context (local bank names, local payment systems) |
| Cannot measure behaviour change | Behaviour tracking dashboard | Tracks individual and team click rates over time, showing trend lines rather than point-in-time snapshots |

## Gain Creators (mapped to gains)
| Gain | Gain Creator | How it works |
|------|-------------|--------------|
| Pass NIS2 audit | Compliance reporting module | Generates NIS2-mapped reports showing programme coverage, completion rates, and improvement trends. Export to PDF for auditors. |
| Reduce phishing click rates | Adaptive difficulty engine | Simulations get progressively harder as employees improve. Employees who click get immediate micro-training. Repeated exposure builds recognition skills. |
| Free up IT security staff time | Campaign automation | Set campaign parameters once. Platform handles scheduling, delivery, tracking, and follow-up training without manual intervention. |

## ROI Ladder
- **Feature:** Adaptive phishing simulations with difficulty that scales based on employee performance
- **Capability:** Security teams can run continuous, progressively challenging phishing tests without manual campaign setup
- **Benefit:** Employees get realistic, localised training that actually changes their behaviour, not just a compliance checkbox
- **Outcome:** Phishing click rates drop measurably over 90 days, and the organisation has audit-ready evidence of an active security awareness programme
- **ROI:** Estimated EUR 15-30K/year in avoided incident response costs per prevented phishing breach, plus compliance penalty avoidance under NIS2 (fines up to EUR 10M or 2% of turnover)

## Ad Lib Value Proposition Statement

> Our adaptive phishing simulation platform helps CISOs at mid-market Southeast European banks who need to build NIS2-compliant security awareness programmes by eliminating the manual effort of running phishing tests and providing measurable evidence of employee behaviour change, unlike KnowBe4 which prices for enterprise and delivers generic English-language content with no regional regulatory mapping.

## Evidence Assessment
- **Overall rating:** Moderate
- **Strongest evidence:** The NIS2 compliance trigger. All 8 interviewees confirmed this is a real and pressing concern. Two banks have committed to free pilots specifically because of NIS2 deadlines. This is the strongest signal in the entire proposition.
- **Biggest assumption:** The claim that adaptive difficulty produces better outcomes than static campaigns. This is the core product thesis, but it has not been tested with real employee populations yet. The two pilots will be the first real test. If click rates do not measurably drop during the 90-day pilots, the adaptive engine is a feature, not a differentiator.

## Next Steps
1. Complete both 90-day pilots and collect before/after phishing click rate data. This is the single most important evidence gap. Without pilot results, the ROI claim is an assumption.
2. Validate the language barrier pain point with more specificity. Four interviewees mentioned it, but none quantified how much it reduces training effectiveness. Run a small test: send the same simulation in English and Serbian to a split group and measure click rate difference.
3. Interview 2-3 compliance officers or external auditors to validate that the reporting format actually meets NIS2 audit requirements. The platform generates reports, but nobody has confirmed auditors will accept them.
