Bondcube / The Journey
Year 1

250 LOIs from banks. EUR 5M raised. Built a 30-person team.

Real regulatory catalyst (MiFID II). Genuine market need. But we treated LOIs as validation. They cost nothing to sign.

13 Months

Built on assumptions. No customer testing. Our CTO was suspended by investors.

EUR 2M tech spend building what we assumed they needed. Our investors were an M&A house. This was their first early-stage deal. They had no idea how to support us. We had no idea what we were doing. Massive investor-founder misfit.

Finally Live

New CTO. Product launched. GBP 51BN daily order volume. Revenue starting to come in.

Fixed the front-end, the back-end worked. Transactions happening. Things were finally moving. We needed more money to keep going.

The End

Our customers wanted to buy out the investors. The investors refused. They liquidated us instead.

EUR 5M raised. 30 people. GBP 51BN in volume. Revenue growing. Customers fighting to save us. Killed by investor-founder misfit.

"We built on assumptions.
We chose the wrong investors.
And when it finally started working,
they pulled the plug."

The market was real. The product worked. The revenue was coming.
But we spent 13 months building on assumptions, ran out of money,
and our investors chose liquidation over letting our customers save us.

The Evidence Scale

Strong Someone paid, signed, or changed their behaviour
Moderate Someone committed time or reputation
Weak Someone said they liked it
Assumption You believe it but haven't tested it

250 LOIs = Weak. I treated it as Strong.

NPL Markets / The Journey
First

Started by mapping our risks. What were the biggest assumptions that could kill us?

Applied every lesson from Bondcube. Before spending a penny, we identified the riskiest assumptions and built a plan to test them.

Then

Interviewed 200 people in the market. Not pitching. Asking about their world.

Banks, investors, servicers, lawyers. We asked how they bought and sold non-performing loans today, what was broken, and what would make them switch.

Then

Used those insights to design our value proposition, competitive positioning, and go-to-market. Evidence-first.

Every decision was grounded in what we heard from the market. Not what we assumed. Not what felt right. What the data told us.

Month 3

First product live. Concierge MVP, matching buyers and sellers by hand.

No platform. No automation. We delivered the value manually to prove the model worked before writing real code.

Year 1

Wave 1 expansion. 10 banks + 150 investors. GBP 500K angel round.

Dominated one niche first. Built credibility in Greek NPLs, then expanded from there. Wave-based growth, not spray and pray.

Year 2

Wave 2. 50 banks + 400 investors. GBP 2M Series A.

But co-founder departed at growth stage. Building the team for scale is a different skill than building for zero to one.

Three fits to prove before you build

1
Customer / Problem Fit
Does this customer have this problem badly enough to pay?
2
Problem / Solution Fit
Does your solution actually solve it?
3
Revenue Model Fit
Can you charge for it sustainably?

Most technical founders skip straight to #2.

THE FOUNDER NIGHTMARES

What kills you at pre-seed

1
Ego Echo-Chamber

2
Quicksand of No Validation
Skipping customer discovery entirely
3
Swamp of False Signals
Mistaking interest for commitment
4
Frankensoft
Trying to build for every customer segment
5
Boardroom Hell
Picking the wrong investors

Bondcube hit all five. The exercises today are designed to catch them early.
