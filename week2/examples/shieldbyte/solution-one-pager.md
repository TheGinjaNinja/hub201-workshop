# ShieldByte
**NIS2-compliant phishing simulation and security awareness training for Southeast European banks.**

## The Problem
Mid-market banks in Serbia face the same NIS2 security awareness requirements as multinational institutions, but lack the teams and tools to comply. Most have no formal programme. IT security managers run occasional phishing tests manually using generic tools, track results in spreadsheets, and deliver training in English to staff who work in Serbian. When auditors ask for evidence of an active awareness programme, there is nothing to show.

## Who This Is For
CISOs and IT Security Managers at Serbian and Southeast European banks (200-1000 employees) facing NIS2 compliance deadlines and needing a documented, measurable security awareness programme.

## What Changes
Before: your team spends hours configuring manual phishing tests with no tracking, no follow-up training, and no audit trail. Employees ignore English-language training modules. Click rates stay flat.

After: automated phishing simulations run continuously in Serbian, adapting difficulty as employees improve. Staff who click receive immediate micro-training in their language. You report measurable click rate trends to your board and hand auditors a NIS2-mapped compliance report.

## How It Works
- Deploys pre-built Serbian-language phishing simulations based on real regional attack patterns. No manual campaign setup.
- Adaptive engine increases simulation difficulty as employees improve, preventing "training fatigue" from repetitive easy tests.
- Employees who fail a simulation get a 3-minute micro-training module explaining what they missed, delivered immediately in Serbian.
- Compliance dashboard tracks click rates, training completion, and improvement trends. Exports NIS2-mapped audit reports.

## Proof
- Running 90-day pilots with two Serbian banks (300 and 500 employees). Measuring baseline phishing click rates against end-of-pilot rates. Results expected Q3 2026.
- Prototype tested internally with the founding team's former colleagues at a Serbian bank's IT department. Early feedback: "Finally something in our language that our staff will actually read."
- Founded by two CS graduates who spent 3 years in a Serbian bank's IT security team. They built the first simulations based on real phishing attacks they handled in that role.

## Evidence Assessment
**Rating: Emerging.** Two pilots are underway but have not yet produced quantitative results. The strongest evidence is the NIS2 trigger (confirmed across 8 interviews) and the two pilot commitments (time on the Commitment Ladder, not yet money). The weakest link is the "What Changes" section. The claim that click rates will drop and employees will improve is the product thesis, not a proven outcome. Until pilot data comes in, this one-pager is a credible hypothesis, not a validated offer.

What would strengthen it:
- Pilot completion with before/after click rate data. Even preliminary 30-day data would add substance.
- One customer quote from a pilot bank's CISO or IT Security Manager, with permission to use their name.
- Validation from a compliance auditor that the reporting format meets NIS2 requirements.

## Coaching Notes
The founders initially led with "AI-powered adaptive phishing engine" as the headline. Reframed to lead with the NIS2 compliance problem, which is the trigger that makes banks care right now. The AI is in the "How It Works" section where it belongs, described as a mechanism, not the headline.

The "What Changes" section was originally a feature list disguised as outcomes. Pushed the founders to describe the customer's changed reality instead. The before/after framing forces outcome language.

The Proof section is honestly thin. Two unpaid pilots and an internal test. The founders need to resist the temptation to oversell this. "Results expected Q3 2026" is honest. "Proven to reduce click rates" would be a lie at this stage.

Biggest risk: if pilot results are inconclusive or show no measurable improvement, the entire one-pager needs rewriting. The founders should have a plan for what happens if the pilots do not produce the expected results.
