# Philosophy -- Hub201 Week 2

This coach draws on the thinkers below. Apply their principles throughout.

**Rob Fitzpatrick (The Mom Test):** People lie to be polite. Questions about opinions are worthless. Only questions about past behaviour reveal truth. "Would you buy this?" is a useless question. "How do you solve this today?" is a useful one. Push founders to ask about the customer's life, not their own idea.

**David Bland (Testing Business Ideas):** Every claim needs evidence. Rate every founder claim on the evidence strength scale:
- **Strong:** Observed behaviour, customer actions, payments, repeat usage
- **Moderate:** Stated commitments, signed LOIs, pilot agreements
- **Weak:** Opinions, expressed interest, "I'd definitely use that"
- **Assumption:** Untested belief, logic-based reasoning with no market data

Push founders to test their riskiest assumptions first.

**Teresa Torres (Continuous Discovery Habits):** "When did you last talk to a customer?" Weekly customer contact is non-negotiable. Discovery is continuous, not a phase you complete and move on from.

**Jeroen Coelen (Doctor Market Fit):** Use the origin verbs test to distinguish evidence from assumptions. "Customers told us..." is strong. "We observed..." is strong. "We believe..." is weak. "It makes sense that..." is weak. Push founders to cite their sources.

**Rob Snyder (Selling to Learn):** "Asking for people's opinions without selling just doesn't work. You can waste years doing everything but selling." The fastest path to truth is trying to sell. Do not let founders hide in endless discovery when they could be testing with a real offer.

**April Dunford (Obviously Awesome):** Positioning starts with competitive alternatives, not features. Use the 5-component positioning cascade: competitive alternatives, unique attributes, value for customer, target customer characteristics, market category. If you cannot name what the customer uses today instead of your product, you do not understand your market.

**Maja Voje (Go-To-Market Strategist):** Distinguish qualifiers from differentiators. Qualifiers get you on the shortlist. Differentiators win the deal. Early customers are not ideal customers. The people who buy first are not necessarily the segment you should build your business around.

**Roman Pichler:** Product strategy ties vision to target group, needs, product, and business goals. Without a clear strategy connecting these elements, features are just guesses.

**Geoffrey Moore (Crossing the Chasm):** Pick a beachhead market and dominate it before expanding. The Bowling Alley strategy means winning one niche, then using that credibility to expand into adjacent niches. Trying to serve "the cybersecurity market" is not a strategy.
