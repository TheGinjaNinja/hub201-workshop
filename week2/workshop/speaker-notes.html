16:37 Slide 7: Evidence Strength Scale (1 min)
Advance to Evidence Scale slide.

Evidence Strength Scale:

Strong Someone paid, signed, or changed their behaviour
Moderate Someone committed time or reputation
Weak Someone said they liked it
Assumption You believe it but haven't tested it
"250 LOIs? That's Weak evidence. I treated it as Strong. That mistake cost EUR 5 million and 30 people their jobs."
"How many of you have people saying they'd use your product? That's the weakest signal on the ladder."

16:39 Slide 9: NPL Markets Research Findings (2 min)
Advance to "Research showed a strong need with high complexity" slide. Walk through the 5 findings.
"After Bondcube, I started NPL Markets completely differently. Before I spent a single pound on product, I mapped our biggest risks. What were the assumptions that could kill us? Then I went and interviewed 200 people in the market. Not 10. Not 50. 200. And what we found was a market with massive pain but high complexity."
"Five problems kept coming up. The market had no structured way to bid on loan portfolios. Legal costs were enormous for every deal. Brokers gave everyone access to the same buyers, so pricing was terrible. There was no data standard, so nobody could price assets properly. And the whole process took so long that most participants just gave up."
"These were not our assumptions. These were their words. This is what 200 interviews gives you. Clarity."
16:41 Slide 10: NPL Markets Journey (2 min)
Advance to NPL Markets Journey slide. Walk through risk mapping, 200 interviews, evidence-first strategy, month 3 product, wave expansion.
"We started by mapping our risks. What were the biggest assumptions that could kill us? Then we interviewed 200 people. Banks, investors, servicers, lawyers. Not pitching. Asking about their world."
"We used those insights to design our value proposition, competitive positioning, and go-to-market. Every decision was grounded in what we heard from the market. Not what we assumed. Not what felt right. What the data told us."

"Year 1, wave 1 expansion. 10 banks, 150 investors. GBP 500K angel round. Dominated one niche first. Built credibility in Greek NPLs, then expanded from there. Year 2, wave 2. 50 banks, 400 investors. GBP 2M Series A."

16:44 Slide 12: Three Pre-Seed Fits (1 min)
Advance to Three Fits slide.

The 3 fits at pre-seed:

  1. Customer/Problem Fit: Does this customer have this problem badly enough to pay?
  2. Problem/Solution Fit: Does your solution actually solve it in a way they value?
  3. Revenue Model Fit: Can you charge for it in a way that builds a business?
"Most technical founders skip straight to number 2. They build the solution before proving the problem exists. That's what I did with Bondcube. NPL Markets worked because I did them in order."
16:45 Slide 13: Founder Nightmares (1 min)
Advance to Founder Nightmares slide. 5 cards visible.
"These are the five things that kill you at pre-seed. The Ego Echo-Chamber, where you build what you want instead of what customers need. The Quicksand of No Validation, where you skip customer discovery entirely. The Swamp of False Signals, where you mistake interest for commitment. Frankensoft, where you try to build for every customer segment. And Boardroom Hell, where you pick the wrong investors."
"Bondcube hit all five. The exercises today are designed to catch them early."
16:46 Slide 14: Early-Stage Mindset (1.5 min)
Advance to "Cultivate an early-stage mindset" slide. 9 principle cards visible.
"These are the nine principles that emerged from my three companies. Let go of your ego. You are not your user. Stay focused on the problem, not the solution. Focus on the customer. Launch and iterate. Be embarrassed by your first product. Sell before you build. Do things that don't scale. Don't get trapped in the build. And be driven by data, not gut feel."
"Most of you are technical. You are comfortable building. The hardest thing I'm going to ask you to do today is to stop building and start listening."
16:47 Slide 15: Shifting Perspective (1 min)
Advance to "Think like your customer" slide. Self-centric vs customer-centric comparison table.
"This is the shift I need you to make. Stop asking 'what can we sell?' Start asking 'what job does our customer need to get done?' Stop asking 'how can we reach customers efficiently?' Start asking 'how do our customers prefer to be addressed?' The left column is about you. The right column is about them. Everything you build today should come from the right column."
