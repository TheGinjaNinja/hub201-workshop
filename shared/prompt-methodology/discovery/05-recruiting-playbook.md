# Recruiting Interview Candidates

## Expectations

Recruiting is the biggest time sink in customer discovery. It starts before the script is written and it takes longer than founders expect every time. The rule is to start recruiting the moment you have a target segment defined, not after the script is ready.

There is a signal embedded in the recruiting process that many founders miss: if you cannot get your target customers to agree to a 30-minute conversation, that tells you something real about your future sales activities. A market that is impossible to reach for research is a market that will be hard to sell into. Pay attention to what the recruiting process reveals about access.

## B2B hacks

**Cold outreach.** LinkedIn, personalised email, and direct phone calls are the primary tools for B2B recruiting. Personalisation is the variable that separates a response from a delete: reference something specific about the person's role, company, or recent activity. Generic messages produce a near-zero response rate. Specific messages ("I saw you spoke at X conference on Y topic and I'm researching that exact area") produce a meaningful uplift.

**Industry events.** Never pay to attend an industry event if you can avoid it. Negotiate free entry in exchange for helping, speaking, or staffing. Events are one of the highest-yield recruiting environments: attendees are self-selected as engaged with the sector, and meeting someone in person before inviting them to an interview creates a warmer relationship.

**Industry panels and working groups.** Joining a working group or contributing to a policy or standards body is one of the highest-leverage recruiting moves available in regulated industries. It is also slow. The NPL Markets example: the CTO joined ESMA's Working Group on NPLs and contributed to industry papers and market data standards. This created access to candidates (hedge funds, banks, servicers) who would have ignored a cold outreach from an unknown startup. If you are in a sector with active working groups, check whether you qualify to participate and get in immediately.

**Advisory networks.** Use advisors, mentors, and programme connections (Hub201, accelerators, incubators) for warm introductions. A warm intro from a shared contact converts to a booked interview at a much higher rate than cold outreach. Do not hoard the ask: advisors expect to open doors.

**Social media.** Lower response rates for B2B than the other channels, but worth a line. LinkedIn posts work best within 30 days of connecting with someone new, while the connection is still recent in their feed memory. Posting about the research topic ("we're doing research on X and looking for 15-minute conversations with [specific job title]") occasionally produces inbound interest from people who see it in their network.

## B2C hacks

**Cold outreach.** Find targets on social media platforms where your segment gathers. Messages must be highly personalised: reference something on their profile, be upfront about what you are doing, and make the ask small (15 minutes, low friction).

**Social media, organic.** Run posts in communities, groups, or forums where your segment is active. Be transparent that you are a founder doing research. Community members are often more willing to help founders than to respond to commercial outreach.

**Social media with a free offer.** Offer something of genuine value in return for the interview. The P.S. Bridal example: offering personalised style advice in exchange for a 20-minute conversation. The offer was directly relevant to the target segment (brides), had real value, and required no financial outlay. Think about what you know that your target segment would want, then offer it.

**Social media with money.** Offer a small cash incentive (see Incentives section below) and promote the offer through paid social. Limit submissions so you do not overcommit. This is faster to spin up than organic and gives you control over volume, but costs money and can attract respondents who are there for the incentive rather than genuine engagement. Screen them carefully.

**Events.** Organising your own small event (a panel, a workshop, a breakfast briefing) positions you as a credible player in the space and naturally generates interest from exactly the people you want to interview. Speaking slots at others' events have a similar effect. Even a short conference talk creates inbound interest from attendees who identify with the topic.

## Paid platforms and agencies

When self-recruiting is too slow or the segment is too hard to reach directly, paid platforms can accelerate:

- **Respondent**: specialist in B2B recruiting, higher quality respondents for professional roles
- **User Interviews**: broader pool, good for B2C and early B2B
- **Askable**: strong in Australian and UK markets, fast turnaround

Agency options for more established programmes include UserTesting, UsabilityHub, and dScout. These are built for usability research rather than discovery interviews but can work for both.

The cost is typically £20 to £50 per interview. This is worth it at speed, but flag it as a later-stage option. If a founder cannot recruit 10 target customers themselves, that is a concerning signal about the market before spending money on a platform. Use the recruiting process as a sales rehearsal, not an obstacle to outsource.

## Recruiting existing customers

If you are building on top of an existing product or company, existing customers are the fastest and richest source of discovery candidates. They already have context. They already have opinions. They are already in the problem space.

Use this approach for updating existing products, testing usability with power users, or when the research requires product familiarity to be meaningful. Access existing customers through the relationship that is closest to them: the sales rep who closed them, the advisor who referred them, or in early-stage companies, the CEO.

The discipline here is to keep it a research conversation, not a support or sales conversation. Existing customers will pull the discussion toward their current experience of the product. Steer back to the underlying job they are trying to do and the pains that exist in the wider workflow.

## Incentives

£20 to £50 Amazon vouchers is the standard range for B2B interviews. The lower end is appropriate for shorter sessions (15-20 minutes); the higher end for longer sessions (45-60 minutes) or senior respondents whose time is costly.

Sometimes free works better than a voucher if the offer is intrinsically valuable. The P.S. Bridal style advice example produced strong response rates because brides wanted the advice, not the voucher. Jamie's Help 100 Founders challenge produced inbound interest from founders who wanted coaching, not cash. The key is whether the free offer is genuinely valuable to the specific segment.

Offer extra incentives for referrals. "If you know someone else who deals with this problem and would be willing to talk to us, we'll send you an extra voucher." This turns your early interviewees into a recruiting network and is often the fastest way to reach the second and third waves of candidates.
