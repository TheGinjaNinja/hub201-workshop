# Cybersecurity Startup Context

## Common Cybersecurity Buyer Types

- **CISO (Chief Information Security Officer):** Cares about risk reduction, compliance posture, and board-level reporting. Signs off on strategic security purchases.
- **IT Security Manager:** Focused on operational efficiency, tool consolidation, and reducing alert fatigue. Day-to-day user of security tools.
- **Compliance Officer:** Driven by regulatory adherence, audit readiness, and documentation. Will block purchases that don't support compliance workflows.
- **IT Director / CTO:** Evaluates integration with existing stack and total cost of ownership. Wants minimal disruption to current infrastructure.
- **Procurement:** Runs vendor risk assessments and checks certification requirements (ISO 27001, SOC 2). Can delay or kill deals on process alone.

## Typical B2B Cybersecurity Buying Process

- Enterprise sales cycles run 6-18 months. SME cycles are shorter at 1-3 months.
- Multiple stakeholders are involved: technical evaluation, security review, procurement, and legal all get a vote.
- POC/pilot stages are common and expected. Budget for the engineering time to support them.
- Compliance and certification requirements can gate purchasing decisions entirely. No cert, no deal.
- Budget cycles matter. Q4 budget allocation and fiscal year planning drive timing of purchases.
- Internal champions must sell your product inside their organisation before you can close the deal.

## Common Product Categories

- **Endpoint protection (EDR/XDR):** Detection and response on devices and across multiple security layers.
- **SIEM/SOAR:** Security operations, log aggregation, alerting, and automated response workflows.
- **Phishing simulation and security awareness training:** Employee-facing tools to reduce human risk.
- **Vulnerability management and penetration testing:** Finding and prioritising weaknesses before attackers do.
- **Identity and access management (IAM/PAM):** Controlling who can access what, especially privileged accounts.
- **Compliance automation and GRC:** Governance, risk, and compliance tooling to streamline audits and reporting.
- **Threat intelligence platforms:** Aggregating and operationalising threat data for proactive defence.
- **Cloud security (CSPM, CWPP):** Securing cloud configurations and workloads across multi-cloud environments.
- **Email security and anti-phishing:** Filtering malicious emails and preventing business email compromise.
- **Data loss prevention (DLP):** Preventing sensitive data from leaving the organisation.

## Regulatory Landscape

- **GDPR:** EU data protection regulation. Affects any company handling EU citizen data, regardless of where the company is based.
- **NIS2 Directive:** EU network and information security directive with expanding scope through 2024-2026. Significant for critical infrastructure and digital service providers.
- **ISO 27001:** Information security management standard. Often required by enterprise buyers as a baseline for vendor selection.
- **SOC 2:** US-origin audit framework, increasingly expected globally for SaaS providers selling to enterprise.
- **Serbia-specific:** Law on Information Security, national CERT coordination, and ongoing alignment with EU acquis as part of Serbia's EU accession process.
- **PCI DSS:** Payment card industry standard. Relevant for startups selling to fintech, banking, or e-commerce customers.

## Market Dynamics

- **Consolidation trend:** Large vendors are acquiring point solutions to build integrated platforms. Startups should plan for acquisition as a realistic exit path.
- **Platform vs. point solution:** Buyers want fewer vendors but still choose best-of-breed for critical functions. Winning requires being clearly superior in a narrow area.
- **AI-native disruption:** New startups are building AI-first security tools, but "we use AI" alone is not differentiation in 2026. Every vendor claims AI.
- **Managed security services (MSSP/MDR) growth:** Many SMEs outsource security entirely. Partnering with MSSPs can be a distribution channel.
- **SME underserved segment:** Most enterprise tools are overpriced and overcomplicated for 200-1000 employee companies. This is a real gap.
- **Regional opportunity:** Southeast Europe and Western Balkans are underserved by global security vendors, creating space for local and regional players.
- **Talent shortage:** The global cybersecurity workforce gap means tools that reduce analyst workload have strong value propositions.

## Common Mistakes Cybersecurity Startups Make

- **Building without understanding the buyer journey.** Know who signs the cheque, not just who uses the tool. Technical admiration does not equal a purchase order.
- **Underestimating enterprise sales cycles.** Expecting SaaS-speed sales in a 12-month procurement process leads to cash flow crises.
- **Ignoring compliance as a market wedge.** Compliance requirements create urgency that pure-play security tools often lack. Regulatory deadlines are real deadlines.
- **"We use AI" as differentiation.** The question buyers ask is "AI applied to what problem, with what data, producing what outcome?" Answer that or drop the claim.
- **Competing with incumbents on features.** You will not out-feature CrowdStrike or Palo Alto. Find a workflow they ignore or a segment they underserve.
- **Targeting "all companies" instead of a beachhead segment.** Pick a specific buyer persona, company size, and industry vertical. Expand from there.
- **Building a demo, not a product.** An impressive POC that cannot handle production workloads will stall at the pilot stage every time.
- **Pricing mismatch.** Pricing like enterprise when selling to SME kills deals. Pricing like SME when selling to enterprise signals immaturity.
