# Evidence and Commitment Scales

Two scales that every GNV coach applies consistently across all workshops and exercises. Use them to rate founder claims, guide coaching conversations, and show teams what stronger evidence looks like.

## Evidence Strength Scale

| Level | Meaning | Examples |
|-------|---------|---------|
| Strong | Observed behaviour or actions | Customer paid, signed contract, repeated usage, measurable workflow change |
| Moderate | Stated commitments | Signed LOI with date, agreed to pilot with budget allocated |
| Weak | Opinions or interest | "I'd use that", positive feedback in interviews, LinkedIn likes |
| Assumption | Untested belief | "We think CISOs need this", market size from a report with no customer validation |

## The Commitment Ladder

Use this to test the depth of founder claims about customer interest. "Customers are excited" could mean anything. Push teams to identify the highest rung they have actually reached.

1. **Attention** - will take a meeting (weak signal)
2. **Time** - will do a trial or pilot (moderate)
3. **Reputation** - will refer you or go on record (moderate to strong)
4. **Money** - will pay (strong)
5. **Repeat** - will pay again (validated)

If a founder says "customers are interested," probe for the specific commitment level. Interest alone is the weakest signal on the ladder, but it is a starting point. Coach them toward the next rung.

## How coaches use these scales

Rate every founder claim as it comes up. Say the rating out loud. "That is an assumption." "That is weak evidence." "That is strong, well done." Use the rating as a coaching signal, not a gate.

The scales are information, not verdicts. A claim rated "assumption" does not mean the work stops. It means the team now knows exactly what they are building on and what they need to go test. A claim rated "strong" tells the team they have earned a confident assertion in their outputs.

When an output is generated, every major claim should carry a visible evidence rating. Teams should be able to look at their own output and see, at a glance, what is grounded and what is still a hypothesis.
