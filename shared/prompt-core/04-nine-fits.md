# The 9 Fits Model

Credit: Jamie Reynolds' Startup Operating Model at Ginger Ninja Ventures. This is the map from idea to Series A.

Nine distinct fits that a startup must validate in sequence. Get them in the wrong order and you build something that does not scale, cannot raise, or falls apart under growth. They are not milestones to tick off. They are recurring questions you keep answering with better evidence as you go.

## Pre-seed - three fits

**Customer/Problem Fit** - does this customer segment have this problem badly enough to pay for a solution? Evidence: 10-30 discovery interviews with consistent pain signals, behavioural not just stated. The problem must be "hair on fire" severity. If customers can live with it or have a workaround they are satisfied with, you do not have this fit yet.

**Problem/Solution Fit** - does your proposed solution solve the problem in a way the customer values? Evidence: concierge MVP or prototype tested with real users, willingness to pay even small amounts, repeat usage or requests for more. Not "they liked it in a demo" but "they came back and used it again, or asked for a next session."

**Revenue Model Fit** - can you charge for this sustainably? Evidence: unit economics that work at customer level, pricing validated with real transactions. You do not need scale yet. You need one clean proof point that the model works for one customer, then a plausible path to repeating it.

## Seed - four fits

**Market/Channel Fit** - a repeatable channel that reaches your segment economically. You are not experimenting with five things simultaneously. You have found one channel that works and you understand why it works.

**Team/Market Fit** - founders and early hires who understand and can reach this segment. Do you have the relationships, the domain credibility, or the lived experience to open doors and earn trust in this market? If not, who do you need on board?

**GTM Fit** - a sales motion that closes deals repeatably. You know your ICP, your outreach, your demo, your objection handling, and your close. You can teach it to someone else.

**Unit Economics Fit** - LTV beats CAC by a meaningful margin, payback period is acceptable to the business and to investors, and the gross margin structure is viable at scale. Not modelled. Measured.

## Series A - two fits

**Scale Fit** - the machine works without the founder's attention on every deal. You have process, team, and repeatability. Growth does not depend on you being in every room.

**Capital Fit** - the business model absorbs capital productively into growth, not into fixing problems that should have been solved at seed. Investors putting money in accelerates the flywheel. It does not patch the leaky bucket.

## Which fit does this workshop address?

Each week targets one fit explicitly. Teams should know which fit they are working on and what evidence would move them from assumption to validated.

- Week 2 focuses on Problem/Solution Fit (positioning and differentiation).
- Week 3 focuses on Customer/Problem Fit (discovery, ICP definition, interview quality).

If a team does not yet have evidence of the fit this week addresses, the workshop is how they start collecting it. Do not send them back to gather data before engaging. Help them design the next steps that will get them the evidence they need, and produce outputs that show their current best understanding with gaps clearly labelled.
