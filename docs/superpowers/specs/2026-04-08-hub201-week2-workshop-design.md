### Slide 2: Bondcube (6 min) - "Evidence is not interest"

**Speaker notes:**

"My first startup was Bondcube. 2012. B2B fintech for capital markets. We were going to revolutionise how bonds were traded.

I raised EUR 5 million. I hired 30 people. We built for 13 months. We had 250 organisations that said they wanted our product. 250 letters of intent.

And then we launched. And nothing happened. Not one of those 250 organisations paid. Not one converted to a customer. Zero revenue.

Meanwhile, a competitor shipped faster, simpler, cheaper. We were liquidated.

I had confused interest with evidence. People saying 'yes, I'd use that' is the weakest signal you can get. It costs them nothing to say yes. The only evidence that matters is behaviour. Did they sign? Did they pay? Did they come back?

So here's a framework I wish I'd had back then."

**[Introduce the Evidence Strength Scale]**

"Every claim you make about your startup sits on this scale:
- Strong: someone paid, signed, or changed their behaviour
- Moderate: someone committed time or reputation
- Weak: someone said they liked it
- Assumption: you believe it but haven't tested it

250 LOIs? That's Weak evidence. I treated it as Strong. That mistake cost EUR 5 million and 30 people their jobs.

How many of you have people saying they'd use your product? That's the weakest signal on the ladder. I'm not saying you're wrong. I'm saying you don't know yet."

**Key points to hit:**
- EUR 5M raised, 30 people, 13 months, 250 LOIs, zero revenue, liquidated
- The punchline: confused interest with evidence
- Introduce evidence strength scale (Strong/Moderate/Weak/Assumption)
- Connect to their world: "How many of you have people saying they'd use your product?"

### Slide 3: NPL Markets (5 min) - "Speed of discovery beats speed of building"

**Speaker notes:**

"After Bondcube, I swore I'd never make those mistakes again. In 2018 I founded NPL Markets. Non-performing loan data for banks and investors.

Same founder. Completely different approach.

This time I talked to customers before I built anything. I ran 100 discovery conversations in 3 months. Not pitching. Not selling. Asking about their world. How do you buy non-performing loan portfolios today? What's broken about that process? What does it cost you?

The result: 100 letters of intent AND a working product in 3 months. GBP 500K angel round. Then a GBP 2 million Series A. We expanded to 50 banks and 400 investors.

What changed? I was obsessed with proving that the customer had the problem before I built the solution. I was testing what I call Customer/Problem Fit before touching Problem/Solution Fit.

At pre-seed, there are three fits you need to prove:
1. Customer/Problem Fit: does this customer have this problem badly enough to pay?
2. Problem/Solution Fit: does your solution actually solve it in a way they value?
3. Revenue Model Fit: can you charge for it in a way that builds a business?

Most technical founders skip straight to number 2. They build the solution before proving the problem exists. That's what I did with Bondcube. NPL Markets worked because I did them in order."

**Key points to hit:**
- Same founder, different approach
- 100 discovery conversations before building
- GBP 500K angel, GBP 2M Series A, 50 banks, 400 investors
- Introduce the 3 pre-seed fits (Customer/Problem, Problem/Solution, Revenue Model)
- "Most technical founders skip straight to Problem/Solution Fit"

### Slide 4: P.S. Bridal (4 min) - "Do the thing that doesn't scale"

**Speaker notes:**

"Third startup. P.S. Bridal. 2022. Wedding dress rental.

Completely different industry. But I applied everything I'd learned.

GBP 5,000 in tech costs. One month to market. Three people.

I didn't build a platform. I rented wedding dresses out of storage. I did the customer service myself. I packed the boxes myself. I wanted to prove that people would rent a wedding dress before I built any technology.

They did. 8,500 customers. GBP 5 million Series A.

The lesson: you don't need to build an AI engine to prove your cybersecurity startup works. You need to prove someone has the problem badly enough to pay. That might mean running a manual phishing simulation by hand for three companies. It might mean doing a security audit with a spreadsheet. Whatever it takes to get evidence of the problem before you invest months building the solution.

The arc of my three companies is this: expensive ignorance with Bondcube, disciplined experimentation with NPL Markets, radical simplicity with P.S. Bridal. I want you to start at the end of that arc, not the beginning."

**Key points to hit:**
- GBP 5K tech, 1 month to market, 3 people, 8,500 customers, GBP 5M Series A
- "I didn't build a platform. I rented dresses out of storage."
- Connect to cybersecurity: "You don't need to build an AI engine"
- The three-venture arc: expensive ignorance, disciplined experimentation, radical simplicity

### Slide 5: The Framework (3 min) - "Here's what you're going to do today"

**Speaker notes:**

"So here's where we are. Most of you are in solution mode. You've got an idea, you're building it, and you're assuming customers will want it. That's Bondcube thinking.

Today we're pulling you back to customer and problem mode.

You're going to work with an AI startup coach. It will walk you through 6 exercises: value proposition, competition matrix, differentiation statement, solution one-pager, personas, and a sales battlecard.

The coach will ask you tough questions. It will challenge vague answers. And it will rate every claim you make on that evidence scale: Strong, Moderate, Weak, or Assumption.

Be honest with it. If you don't have evidence, say so. The exercises are designed to expose your gaps, not hide them. That's how they help you.

At the end, you'll have a professional HTML presentation that shows where your evidence is strong and where you need to go and do more discovery. That's your deliverable. That's what we'll review together in our 1:1 sessions."

**Key points to hit:**
- "Most of you are in solution mode. Today we pull you back to customer and problem mode."
- Brief overview of the 6 exercises
- The AI coach will challenge them
- Evidence ratings on every claim
- Deliverable: HTML presentation for 1:1 review

## Block 2: Q&A (16:50-17:05)

**Speaker notes:**

Open the floor. 15 minutes. If questions are slow to start, seed with:

"Let me ask you something first. How many of you have spoken to more than 10 potential customers about the problem you're solving? Not pitching. Not demoing. Just asking about their world."

(Expect very few hands. Use this to reinforce the message.)

"That's normal for technical founders. And that's exactly what we're going to start fixing today."

**If common questions come up:**

- "How is this different from a business model canvas?" - "A canvas is a map. These exercises are evidence tests. Every section forces you to rate whether your claims are backed by data or by hope."
- "What if we're already on the market?" - "Great. Then you should have Strong evidence for most of your claims. Let's see if you do."
- "We can't share our technical approach with competitors in this cohort." - "You don't need to. The coach works with you individually. None of your outputs are shared. The exercises are about your customer and your market positioning, not your codebase."

## Block 3: Exercise Setup (17:05-17:20)

### Part 1: "What good looks like" (7 min)

**Speaker notes:**

Screen-share the ProcureSimple HTML presentation (examples/procuresimple/presentation.html on GitHub Pages).

"Before you start, let me show you what a completed set of exercises looks like. This is ProcureSimple, a fictional procurement startup. It's not cybersecurity, deliberately, so you can focus on the format, not the domain.

[Scroll to Value Proposition section]

See the persona card? 'Spreadsheet-Drowning Karel.' Notice every claim has an evidence rating. 'No consolidated view of supplier spend. Evidence: 5 of 6 interviewees confirmed this. Rating: Moderate.' That's what I want from you. Specific claims with honest evidence ratings.

[Scroll to the Ad Lib statement]

This is the value proposition statement. One sentence that captures who you serve, what pain you solve, and how you're different. If you can't fill in every blank, that tells you where your gaps are.

[Scroll to Competition Matrix]

Look at the scoring grid. ProcureSimple scored themselves a 1 on contract management and a 1 on ERP integration. That's a pre-seed startup being honest about their gaps. They scored a 5 on SME pricing because that's genuinely their edge. If you score yourself 5 on everything, the AI coach will call you out. And if it doesn't, I will in our 1:1."

**Key points to hit:**
- Use ProcureSimple, not ShieldByte (avoids competitive sensitivity)
- Show evidence ratings on claims
- Show honest low scores on the competition grid
- "If you score yourself 5 on everything, the coach will call you out"

### Part 2: "How the AI coach works" (5 min)

**Speaker notes:**

"Here's how you're going to work for the next hour.

[Share the repo link in chat: https://github.com/TheGinjaNinja/hub201-workshop]

You have two options. If you're comfortable with a terminal, clone this repo and run Claude Code. There's a setup guide in the repo that walks you through it step by step, even if you've never used a terminal before.

If you'd rather not install anything, go to claude.ai, start a conversation, and paste the contents of the CLAUDE.md file from the repo. The link is in the README.

Either way, the AI coach will introduce itself, ask about your startup, ask for your branding, and then present the 6 exercises. Pick one and it walks you through it one question at a time.

It will challenge you. It will rate your evidence. It will tell you when your answers are vague. That is the point."

**Key points to hit:**
- Drop the repo link in chat
- Two tracks: terminal or web
- The coach asks questions one at a time, challenges weak answers
- It will ask for their branding so outputs look like theirs

### Part 3: "Your mission for the next hour" (3 min)

**Speaker notes:**

"Here's your mission. Start with Value Proposition. If you finish that, move to Competition Matrix, then Differentiation Statement. Those three are the core.

I'll check in with you every 15-20 minutes. I'll ask what surprised you and where you're stuck. You don't need to share your answers with the group. I just want to know you're making progress.

Start now. Get the coach running. If you hit any setup issues, drop them in the chat and I'll help."

**Key points to hit:**
- Priority order: Value Proposition first, then Competition Matrix, then Differentiation
- Check-ins every 15-20 min
- No need to share answers publicly
- "Start now"

## Block 4: Teams Working with Check-ins (17:20-18:15)

### Check-in 1 (17:35, ~15 min in)

**Purpose:** Logistics. Make sure everyone is running.

**Speaker notes:**

"Quick check. Type in the chat: what exercise are you working on?

Anyone stuck on setup? Drop your issue in the chat.

For those of you already in the value proposition exercise: has the coach asked you about your target customer yet? If it told you your answer was too vague, good. That means it's working."

**If many are stuck on setup:** Walk through the web-based option live. "Go to claude.ai. Copy the CLAUDE.md contents. Paste it. Start talking to it."

### Check-in 2 (17:50, ~30 min in)

**Purpose:** The teaching moment. Most teams have had claims challenged by now.

**Speaker notes:**

"Quick check-in. How many of you have been told that something you said is an Assumption? Type yes or no in the chat.

[Wait for responses. Expect most to say yes.]

Good. That's the exercise working. Let me tell you why this matters.

When I had 250 LOIs at Bondcube, I thought I had evidence. I had assumptions dressed up as evidence. The coach is doing for you in 30 minutes what it took me EUR 5 million to learn.

Quick question for anyone willing to share: what was the most surprising thing the coach challenged you on?"

[If someone shares, use it to reinforce. If nobody shares, that's fine.]

"If nobody wants to share, that's okay. But here's what I see across every cohort I work with: the first time founders honestly rate their evidence, they discover that 80% of their value proposition is built on assumptions. That's not bad news. That's a map of what you need to go and validate."

### Check-in 3 (18:05, ~45 min in)

**Purpose:** Wind down. Prime for homework.

**Speaker notes:**

"Last 10 minutes. If you're in the middle of an exercise, keep going. The coach saves your work.

If you've finished an exercise, look at the evidence assessment at the bottom. How many of your claims are Strong or Moderate? How many are Weak or Assumption?

That ratio is the most important thing you'll take away from today. It tells you exactly how much customer discovery you still need to do before your positioning is credible.

Keep working until 18:15. I'll bring you back for a quick wrap-up."

## Block 5: Wrap-up (18:15-18:20)

**Speaker notes:**

"Here's what I want you to take away from today.

You came in thinking about your solution. The exercises are designed to pull you back to your customer and their problem. If the coach told you most of your claims are assumptions, that is not failure. That is clarity. Now you know what to go and validate.

Your homework: complete all 6 exercises before our 1:1 sessions. The AI coach is available any time. You don't need to finish tonight. Use it over the next few days.

When you've completed the exercises, the coach will generate an HTML presentation using your branding. That's your deliverable. That's what we'll review together in the 1:1s.

Zarko will share the booking link for 1:1 sessions. Each session is 30 minutes. I'll review your outputs, challenge your assumptions further, and help you figure out what to validate next.

The repo is yours to keep. The AI coach doesn't expire. Use it after this programme. Use it for Week 3 when we cover customer discovery and ICP.

Any final questions? No? Good work today. Go finish your exercises."

**Key points to hit:**
- Core takeaway: clarity on assumptions, not failure
- Homework: complete all 6 exercises, generate HTML presentation
- 1:1 booking via Zarko
- Repo is theirs to keep
- Week 3 preview (customer discovery)
