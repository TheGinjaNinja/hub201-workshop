# Exercise 1 -- Hypotheses

## Purpose

Turn the team's assumptions about their customer and problem into a prioritised, testable set of hypotheses. By the end of the exercise, the team should know which assumptions they are testing in their interviews, in what order of risk, and what evidence would kill or validate each one.

## Inputs

- Week 2 outputs if available: persona, value proposition, competition matrix.
- The team's current best statement of who has the problem and why.
- Any existing customer evidence, even anecdotal.

## Coaching flow

1. Pull in Week 2 persona and value proposition if present. If Week 2 outputs are missing or thin, help the team draft a first-pass one-liner: "We believe [segment] has [problem], which matters because [reason]."
2. List every assumption behind that statement. Keep pushing for implicit beliefs that have not been articulated ("what are you assuming about how they currently solve this?", "what about budget?", "what about who decides?").
3. Group the assumptions into Desirability (do they want it?), Feasibility (can we build it?), and Viability (will they pay?).
4. Prioritise riskiest-first. For each assumption, ask: if this is wrong, does the business survive? The assumptions that would kill the business if wrong are the ones to test first.
5. Take the top 5 and convert each into a PULL hypothesis:
    - **P**opulation: who specifically are we testing this with?
    - **U**nderstanding: what do we believe is true?
    - **L**earning: what experiment will tell us we are right or wrong?
    - **L**imit: what threshold means success?
6. For each hypothesis, define what evidence would kill it and what evidence would validate it. Use the Evidence Strength Scale.

## Output template

Generate as `/outputs/week3/hypotheses.md`:

- **Header:** Startup name, date, exercise name.
- **Current statement:** one paragraph describing the customer and problem as currently understood.
- **Assumption inventory:** a three-column list or table, grouped by D / F / V.
- **Top 5 assumptions ranked by risk:** one-line each, with the reasoning for risk rank.
- **PULL hypothesis for each top 5:** full four-field format.
- **Kill / validate criteria per hypothesis:** what evidence would settle each.
- **Evidence rating per hypothesis:** strong / moderate / weak / assumption, with source.
- **Single biggest risk:** one sentence naming the assumption most likely to kill the business.
- **Evidence Assessment and Next Steps** per the shared output convention.

## Evidence check

Before accepting the output, ask: are these hypotheses testable through an interview, or are they dressed-up feature assumptions? A hypothesis the team cannot run an interview against is not useful. Help them rewrite those into testable form.
