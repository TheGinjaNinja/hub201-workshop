The Foundation

What discovery is

"Discovery is learning what's true, not what you hope is true."

Rob Fitzpatrick, The Mom Test: people lie to be polite. Questions about opinions are worthless. Only questions about past behaviour reveal truth.

The moment you ask "would you use this?" you have made the interview useless. Your customer cannot tell you whether your product will succeed. Only their past behaviour can.

3 / 45
Core Principle

People lie to be polite.

Not maliciously. They just want to be helpful.

What they said

"I would definitely use this."

"This solves a real problem for us."

"We'd be willing to pay for something like this."

What they did

Did not respond to the next email.

Did not show up to the pilot call.

Did not sign the LoI when asked.

Behaviour is truth. Opinions are noise.

A Warning

The pre-seed trap

"We asked everyone. They said yes. Nothing happened."

This is the most common pattern in early-stage failure. The team runs meetings. The meetings feel great. The pipeline looks full. The product gets built. Then: nobody buys.

This happened to one of my three startups. The numbers were extraordinary. The evidence was worthless. Here is that story.

5 / 45
Story 1: Bondcube

The setup

Institutional B2B fixed income bond trading platform. Buyers: asset managers, hedge funds, and banks. A sophisticated, high-status, well-funded buyer group.

The opportunity

Incumbent systems were fragmented and inefficient. A unified trading layer had genuine potential. The problem space was real.

The mistake

The team mistook enthusiasm for evidence. Every meeting felt like progress. It was not progress. It was politeness.

7 / 45
Story 1: Bondcube

The lesson

Quicksand of No Validation

250 LoIs is not 250 behavioural data points. It is 250 polite yeses. The team never followed up to see whether intent turned into action. They never probed for real experience with adjacent problems.

Ego Echo-Chamber

When everyone in the room agrees with you, you are not learning. You are being entertained. The team was unknowingly setting themselves up for failure.

LoIs without behavioural evidence are a vanity metric. Polite enthusiasm is the weakest signal on the Evidence Strength Scale. Rating it as anything stronger is a failure of discipline.

The first meeting structure

First half: credibility

Present yourself and the concept. Sales posture. Establish why you belong in this conversation. With high-status buyers, this sequencing is not optional.

Second half: discovery

Once credibility is established, earn the right to ask discovery questions. Only then does "tell me about your problems" land.

Credibility buys the meeting. The meeting buys the truth.

Story 3: P.S. Bridal

The setup

B2C personalised wedding dress styling business. Buyers: brides. The challenge: brides will not schedule interviews with strangers. Classical customer interviews simply do not work here.

The constraint

You cannot cold-email a bride and ask for 30 minutes. There is no professional context, no advisory network, no credibility lever.

The insight

Give value first. Make the discovery invisible. Turn every interaction into a data collection event.

15 / 45
Story 3: P.S. Bridal

The trick: tiny value-giving prototypes

Wedding dress style finder

30-question Typeform. The team ran a manual algorithm on responses and sent each bride a personalised style recommendation. In exchange: 40+ data points per bride. Venue, dress size, budget, timing, services needed.

WhatsApp concierge

Direct 1:1 advice. Brides got real help with one of the most important decisions of their lives. The team got candid preferences, concerns, and frustrations in real time.

Every interaction was discovery disguised as value delivery. This is the B2C discovery playbook.

Story 3: P.S. Bridal

The lesson

In B2C, the prototype is the interview. You earn the conversation by giving value first.

A value-giving prototype delivers discovery insights at scale that classical interviews never can.

Iterative prototype ladders compound learning. Each prototype validates or invalidates the last.

Business model fit sometimes comes after value proposition fit. The rental pivot came from the data the prototypes had been collecting all along.

Two years of part-time work produced a strong value proposition and a business model change. Neither was visible at the start.

The Common Arc

What NPL Markets and P.S. Bridal shared

Completely different discovery motions. Same two waypoints: Customer/Problem Fit, then Problem/Solution Fit.

Narrow target. Not "hedge funds" or "brides." Specific sub-segments with specific triggers.

Behaviour over opinion. Real transactions, real usage data, real spend. Not survey answers.

Iteration. Scripts updated after every interview. Prototypes rebuilt after every learning.

Social proof built into the process. ESMA papers for NPL, 8,000 bride data points for P.S. Bridal.

Methodology

The open question rule

Every question must use why, what, or how to surface real experience. Never: do, would, could, or should.

Closed / leading Why it fails Open behavioural rewrite
"Would you use this?" Hypothetical. Always gets yes. "How do you solve this today?"
"Is this a real problem?" Leading. Of course they say yes. "Walk me through the last time this happened."
"Would you pay for this?" Pure hypothetical. Means nothing. "What did you spend on this category last year?"
"Don't you find this frustrating?" You answered it for them. "What part of this process takes the most time?"
"Should we add feature X?" Invites Frankensoft. "What would need to change for this to be worth switching?"

Methodology

The Five Signals diagnostic

Tag every interview question with the signal it elicits. A script heavy on one signal and light on others has blind spots.

Recency

How recent was the last time? Recency confirms the problem is live, not historical.

Concrete behaviour

What did they actually do? Not what they think they might do.

Time and money spent

How long does it take? What do they pay for workarounds? Money spent is the strongest signal.

Problem-area match

Is this problem in their top three? Does it come up unprompted?

Awareness level

Do they know the category exists? Awareness shapes channel and messaging strategy.

24 / 45
Methodology

Value Proposition Canvas: a lens for question design

The VPC (Strategyzer) structures customer context around three lenses. Design questions that explore all three before you touch solutions.

Jobs to be done

  • "What are you trying to accomplish?"
  • "What does success look like for your team this quarter?"
  • "What task takes the most of your attention on a Monday morning?"

Pains

  • "What slows you down most?"
  • "What did you have to redo last week?"
  • "What makes your current process unreliable?"

Gains

  • "What would a good week look like?"
  • "If this was solved, what would it free you up to do?"
  • "What does a happy outcome look like for your team?"

Methodology

The script skeleton

1
Intro
Job title, tenure, context. Establish who you are talking to and how much of the problem they own.
2
Industry
Market context, trends, where they hear about problems in this space. Awareness and positioning questions.
3
Business
The core discovery section. Walk through a recent real incident. Probe tools, process, time, money, who decides.
4
Personal
What takes most of their time? What would they change? The most unguarded answers come here, late in the conversation.
5
Outro
Magic wand closer. Referral ask. Meta-question: "What should I have asked that I didn't?"

Methodology

Cadence, evidence, and commitment

Cadence

  • 5 interviews per week
  • Review script after interview 1
  • Affinity map after interview 10
  • Target 10-20 interviews per segment
  • Never batch write-ups. Do them within 30 minutes.

Evidence Strength Scale

  • Strong: paid, signed, repeated usage
  • Moderate: LoI with date, pilot agreed
  • Weak: "I'd use that," positive feedback
  • Assumption: untested belief, desk research

Commitment Ladder

1AttentionWill take a meeting
2TimeWill do a trial or pilot
3ReputationWill refer you or go on record
4MoneyWill pay
5RepeatWill pay again

Exercise 1

Hypotheses

01

Turn assumptions into PULL hypotheses ranked by risk

List every assumption behind your customer and problem statement. Group by Desirability, Feasibility, Viability. Rank riskiest first. Convert your top 5 into PULL format: Population, Understanding, Learning, Limit. Define what evidence would kill or validate each one.

Output: hypotheses.md in your outputs folder.

The single biggest risk should be named clearly. If you cannot name it, your hypotheses are not honest enough yet.

28 / 45
Exercise 2

Interview Plan

02

Design a script that tests hypotheses without leading

Every question must be behavioural. Tag each question with the hypothesis it tests and the signal it elicits. Include consent script. Add the magic wand closer. Define your cadence (5/week, review after 1, affinity map after 10). Include a post-interview write-up template.

Output: interview-plan.md in your outputs folder.

Check: does every question probe behaviour over opinion? Does the script cover all five signals?
