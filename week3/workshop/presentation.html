The Foundation

What discovery is

"Discovery is learning what's true, not what you hope is true."

Rob Fitzpatrick, The Mom Test: people lie to be polite. Only questions about past behaviour reveal truth. The moment you ask "would you use this?" the interview is useless.

3 / 45
Core Principle

People lie to be polite.

Not maliciously. They just want to be helpful.

What they said

"I would definitely use this."

"This solves a real problem for us."

"We'd be willing to pay for something like this."

What they did

Did not respond to the next email.

Did not show up to the pilot call.

Did not sign the LoI when asked.

Behaviour is truth. Opinions are noise.

A Warning

The pre-seed trap

"We asked everyone. They said yes. Nothing happened."

Meetings feel great. The pipeline looks full. The product gets built. Nobody buys. This happened to one of my three startups. The numbers were extraordinary. The evidence was worthless.

Story 1: Bondcube

The lesson

Quicksand of No Validation

250 LoIs is not 250 behavioural data points. It is 250 polite yeses.

Ego Echo-Chamber

When everyone in the room agrees with you, you are not learning. You are being entertained.

LoIs without behavioural evidence are a vanity metric.

Story 3: P.S. Bridal

The lesson

In B2C, the prototype is the interview. You earn the conversation by giving value first.

Value-giving prototypes deliver discovery at scale that interviews never can.

Each prototype validates or invalidates the last. Iterate, do not plan.

Methodology

The open question rule

Banned words: do, would, could, should. Use why, what, how to surface real experience.

Closed or leading Why it fails Open behavioural rewrite
"Would you use this?" Hypothetical. Always gets yes. "How do you solve this today?"
"Is this a real problem?" Leading. Of course they say yes. "Walk me through the last time this happened."
"Would you pay for this?" Pure hypothetical. Means nothing. "What did you spend on this category last year?"
"Don't you find this frustrating?" You answered it for them. "What part of this process takes the most time?"
"Should we add feature X?" Invites Frankensoft. "What would need to change for this to be worth switching?"

Methodology

The Five Signals diagnostic

Tag every question. No questions for a signal = blind spot.

Recency

When was the last time?

Concrete behaviour

What did you actually do?

Time and money

How long? What did you pay?

Problem-area match

Is this in their top three?

Awareness level

Do they know the category exists?

24 / 45
Methodology

Value Proposition Canvas: question lenses

Strategyzer. Explore all three before you touch solutions.

Jobs to be done

  • "What are you trying to accomplish?"
  • "What does success look like for your team this quarter?"
  • "What task takes the most of your attention on a Monday morning?"

Pains

  • "What slows you down most?"
  • "What did you have to redo last week?"
  • "What makes your current process unreliable?"

Gains

  • "What would a good week look like?"
  • "If this was solved, what would it free you up to do?"
  • "What does a happy outcome look like for your team?"

Methodology

Cadence, evidence, and commitment

Cadence

  • 5 per week
  • Script review after interview 1
  • Affinity map after interview 10
  • Write up within 30 minutes

Evidence Strength

  • Strong: paid, signed, repeated
  • Moderate: LoI with date, pilot agreed
  • Weak: "I'd use that"
  • Assumption: untested belief

Commitment Ladder

1AttentionWill take a meeting
2TimeWill pilot
3ReputationWill refer or go on record
4MoneyWill pay
