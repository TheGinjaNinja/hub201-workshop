## Outreach Templates

All templates are illustrative starting points [illustrative]. Personalise every message before sending. Generic messages produce generic response rates.

### Personalised cold email

Subject: SOC phishing triage at [Company name] -- quick question

> Hi [First name],
>
> I noticed [Company name] recently hired a SOC analyst [or: "recently expanded your security team"] -- which suggests phishing triage is a growing focus for the team.
>
> I am researching how Heads of Security at regulated fintechs in the [DACH / CEE / UK] market are handling phishing response operationally. Specifically whether triage time and false-positive rates are real pain points or manageable today.
>
> Would you be willing to spend 20 minutes sharing how your team handles this? No pitch, just research. Happy to share what I am hearing from others in the space in return.
>
> [Your name]
> ShieldByte

**Length:** 4 sentences maximum. Longer emails get lower response rates.

---

### LinkedIn opener (2 sentences)

> Hi [First name], I came across your profile while researching how security leads at [sector] fintechs handle phishing triage operationally. I am doing 20-minute research interviews this month -- would you be open to a brief chat?

**Note:** Do not mention the product. Do not say "I want to show you something." Ask only for a research conversation.

---

### Cold call script

**Opening:**
"Hi, could I speak to [Name]? My name is [Your name] from ShieldByte."

**Reason for call:**
"I am doing research into how security leads at fintechs your size handle phishing triage operationally, specifically the time burden on SOC teams and how false positives are managed. I want to understand whether this is a real pain point or well-handled. I am not pitching anything."

**Ask:**
"Would you be willing to spend 20 minutes on a call this week? I would be happy to share what I am hearing from others in the space."

**If they say they are busy:**
"Completely understood. Could I send you a one-line email to follow up? I will be brief."

---

### Referral ask (for after a good interview)

Use at the end of every interview where you felt the conversation went well.

> "This has been really helpful, thank you. I am doing 20 of these conversations across the market. Is there anyone else in your network who handles security operations at a similar type of firm that you think I should speak to? Even one name would be really valuable."

**Note:** Ask for a specific name, not a general "yes, probably." If they say yes, follow up with: "Who is the first person that comes to mind?" Let them name someone rather than prompting.

## Channel Mix Recommendation

| Channel | Proportion | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn cold outreach | 40% | Highest volume, lowest response rate. Personalise every message. Use the trigger (recent SOC hire, recent LinkedIn post, recent news) as the opener hook. |
| Industry events (FS-ISAC Europe, CyberSec Europe) [illustrative] | 30% | Higher response rates because you have a shared context. Approach before the event if the agenda is public. Follow up within 48 hours while the memory is fresh. |
| Warm intros (Hub201 advisor and mentor network) | 20% | Highest response rate and best interview quality. Prioritise this channel first. Ask every advisor and mentor at the start of the week. |
| Industry bodies and working groups | 10% | Slow to yield meetings but builds long-term credibility. Being in the room at an FS-ISAC working group makes every subsequent cold outreach land better. |

Adjust channel proportions after week 2 based on actual response rates. If LinkedIn is outperforming events, shift budget toward it. If warm intros are producing better interview quality, invest more time in network expansion.
