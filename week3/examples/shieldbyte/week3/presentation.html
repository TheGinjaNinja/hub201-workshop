Exercise 1

Hypotheses

ShieldByte enters Week 3 with five prioritised, testable hypotheses. All are rated Assumption at this stage. That is expected and honest. The value of this document is in making the assumptions explicit before the team starts interviews.

Illustrative data notice: Every number, price point, company name, event name, industry body, and contact name in this document is labelled [illustrative]. These are coaching examples, not market facts. The team must verify all data with primary sources.

Current problem statement

ShieldByte believes that Heads of Security at 200-500 person regulated European fintechs spend several hours per phishing triage cycle and would pay for automation that materially cuts triage time and reduces false positives. This is an assumption. No interviews have been run yet.

H1 Analyst triage time Assumption

SOC analysts spend 3-5 hours per phishing triage cycle [illustrative]. This is the core ROI premise. If triage time is under 30 minutes, the business case collapses.

Population
SOC analysts and Heads of Security at 200-500 person regulated fintechs in DACH, CEE, or UK who responded to at least one phishing incident in the last three months.
Understanding
We believe analysts spend 3-5 hours [illustrative] per triage cycle: detection, investigation, escalation, and closure.
Learning
Ask interviewees to walk through the most recent phishing incident in detail. Cross-reference triage steps with time estimates from the interviewee directly.
Limit
If 7 of 10 describe triage of under 60 minutes, the ROI story needs rebuilding. If 7 of 10 describe 2+ hours, H1 is directionally validated.
Kill if
Fewer than 3 of 10 interviewees describe triage taking more than 60 minutes.
Validate if
7 or more of 10 describe cycles of 2 hours or more and connect it to missed alerts or delayed response.
H2 Buyer title: Head of Security is the budget holder Assumption

At 200-500 person fintechs, the Head of Security (not CISO, not IT Director) holds the budget for tooling purchases. If wrong, every GTM message is aimed at the wrong person.

Population
200-500 person regulated fintechs in DACH, CEE, or UK with at least one dedicated SOC analyst on staff.
Understanding
The Head of Security holds independent signing authority for tooling purchases at this company size. CISO exists at larger firms; IT Director focuses on infrastructure.
Learning
Ask "who signs off your security tool budget?" and "walk me through the last security tool your team bought" in every interview.
Limit
7 of 10 confirm Head of Security signs off independently. If they route through CISO or CFO, the GTM motion must change.
Kill if
Fewer than 3 of 10 say Head of Security signs off independently.
Validate if
7 or more confirm independent signing authority for tools under a threshold [team to verify].
H3 Price point: GBP 1,000-2,500 per seat per month [illustrative] Assumption

Buyers will pay GBP 1,000-2,500 per seat per month [illustrative]. This is a pricing assumption with zero research behind it. Test by asking about existing spend, not by asking "would you pay X."

Population
Heads of Security at 200-500 person regulated fintechs with existing security tooling budget.
Understanding
Buyers in this segment have budget envelopes in the GBP 1,000-2,500/seat/month range for security tooling [illustrative].
Learning
"What did you spend on security tooling in the last 12 months? Where did it go?" Surfaces existing budget without triggering negotiation mode.
Limit
5 of 10 describe existing spend in the right ballpark and frame the value of triage automation in monetary terms without prompting.
Kill if
More than half describe security tool spend well below GBP 500/seat/month [illustrative] or exclude phishing triage from their budget category.
Validate if
5 or more describe spend in the right ballpark and discuss automation value in monetary terms unprompted.
H4 Analysts would switch for materially lower false-positive rates Assumption

Switching costs in enterprise security tooling are high. A clear measurable improvement is needed to justify replacing an embedded stack. This assumption has not been tested at all.

Population
SOC analysts at regulated fintechs currently using phishing detection tools, standalone or embedded in broader security platforms.
Understanding
False-positive rates are high enough to be a real pain. Analysts would actively switch for a tool that reduced them materially [team to verify what "material" means].
Learning
"What's the false-positive rate on your current tooling?" and "what was the last tool your team adopted that actually stuck? Why?" and "what got kicked out? Why?"
Limit
6 of 10 describe false positives as a significant time drain and describe at least one prior tool switch driven by technical performance.
Kill if
Interviewees describe current tools as good enough, or describe switching costs as prohibitive regardless of performance gain.
Validate if
6 or more describe false positives as a significant time drain and at least one prior switch driven by technical performance.
H5 Onboarding under 60 days at a regulated fintech Assumption

Compliance review, vendor risk assessment, DPIA, and IT approval at a regulated fintech can run to 3-6 months [illustrative]. If the assumption is wrong, cash flow and early revenue models need rethinking.

Population
IT Security and Procurement teams at 200-500 person regulated fintechs in DACH, CEE, or UK.
Understanding
A new security tool can complete vendor approval, compliance sign-off, IT integration, and analyst training in under 60 days at this company size.
Learning
"What's the typical timeline from picking a tool to full deployment?" and "walk me through the last compliance audit your team went through."
Limit
7 of 10 describe prior tool purchases completing within 90 days from selection to live deployment, with compliance sign-off included.
Kill if
More than half describe vendor approval and IT integration alone taking longer than 90 days at a regulated fintech.
Validate if
7 or more describe prior security tool purchases completing within 90 days from selection to live deployment.
Single Biggest Risk

H1 is the single biggest risk. The entire ROI story depends on analyst triage time being long enough that automation creates meaningful value. If the real number is under 30 minutes per cycle, there is no compelling business case and the product category needs rethinking. Every other assumption can be adjusted. If H1 is wrong, the problem framing is wrong.

Red-Flag Question Patterns -- Never Use These

Banned pattern Why it fails Rewrite
"Would you use a tool like this?" Invites hypothetical approval. People say yes to be polite. "How do you handle this today?"
"Don't you find phishing triage really time-consuming?" Leading question. You have answered it for them. "Walk me through the last phishing triage your team handled."
"How important is it to reduce false positives?" Importance question gets an importance answer. No useful data. "What does your team do when an alert turns out to be a false positive?"
"Would you pay for this if it existed?" Pure hypothetical. Means nothing about actual buying behaviour. "What did you spend on security tooling in the last 12 months?"
"Is phishing a big problem at your firm?" Yes/no question, almost always answered yes. No actionable data. "When was the last phishing incident your team triaged? Walk me through it."

Outreach Templates

Personalised cold email [illustrative]

Subject: SOC phishing triage at [Company] -- quick question

Hi [First name],

I noticed [Company] recently hired a SOC analyst -- which suggests phishing triage is a growing focus for the team.

I am researching how Heads of Security at regulated fintechs in [DACH / CEE / UK] handle phishing response operationally. Specifically whether triage time and false-positive rates are real pain points or manageable today.

Would you be willing to spend 20 minutes sharing how your team handles this? No pitch, just research. Happy to share what I am hearing from others in the space in return.

[Your name], ShieldByte

4 sentences maximum. Longer emails get lower response rates. Personalise the opening hook with a specific trigger.

LinkedIn opener (2 sentences) [illustrative]

Hi [First name], I came across your profile while researching how security leads at [sector] fintechs handle phishing triage operationally. I am doing 20-minute research interviews this month -- would you be open to a brief chat?

Do not mention the product. Do not say "I want to show you something." Ask only for a research conversation.

Cold call script [illustrative]

Opening: "Hi, could I speak to [Name]? My name is [Your name] from ShieldByte."

Reason: "I am doing research into how security leads at fintechs your size handle phishing triage operationally -- specifically the time burden on SOC teams and how false positives are managed. I am not pitching anything."

Ask: "Would you be willing to spend 20 minutes on a call this week? I would be happy to share what I am hearing from others in the space."

If busy: "Completely understood. Could I send you a one-line email to follow up?"

Referral ask (after a good interview) [illustrative]

"This has been really helpful, thank you. I am doing 20 of these conversations across the market. Is there anyone else in your network who handles security operations at a similar type of firm that you think I should speak to? Even one name would be really valuable."

Ask for a specific name. If they say yes, follow up: "Who is the first person that comes to mind?"
