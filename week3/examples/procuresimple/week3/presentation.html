ProcureSimple Week 3: Customer Discovery and ICP

Spend management for European mid-sized manufacturers. Three discovery artefacts prepared before the first customer conversation.

Segment: 500-2000 person European manufacturers Date: 2026-04-23 Stage: Pre-seed, entering discovery Evidence level: Assumption-Heavy (expected)
Exercise 1

Hypotheses

Week 2 positioning shifted from Czech SMEs (50-200 person manufacturers) to mid-market European manufacturers in metals, machinery, and food processing (500-2000 employees). Almost all Week 2 evidence needs retesting with this new segment. Five hypotheses ranked by risk. All rated Assumption at this stage.

Biggest Risk: H2 Budget Ownership

If Finance (not Procurement) holds the budget for spend management software at this company size, the entire GTM motion is targeting the wrong person. Procurement-first outreach reaches the champion but not the buyer. This must be tested in the first five interviews before any further GTM investment is made.

H1 Procurement admin time: 30%+ spent chasing suppliers and reconciling invoices [illustrative] Assumption
Population
Heads of Procurement at 500-2000 person European manufacturers in metals, machinery, or food processing managing 50+ active supplier relationships.
Understanding
These buyers spend 30%+ of their time [illustrative] on supplier chasing and invoice reconciliation: following up missing invoices, correcting PO mismatches, chasing delivery confirmations.
Learning
Ask interviewees to walk through their last week at work in detail. How many hours went to supplier admin? How many invoices does the team reconcile per month and how long does each take?
Limit
If 7 of 10 interviewees describe supplier admin as consuming more than 20% of their team's time and cite it as a real constraint, H1 is directionally validated.
Kill if...
Fewer than 3 of 10 describe supplier admin as a meaningful time drain. Most say existing tools or processes are adequate.
Validate if...
7+ of 10 describe supplier admin consuming significant time, express frustration with manual work, and connect it to missed higher-value work.
H2 Finance, not Procurement, holds the budget for spend management software at this company size Biggest Risk
Population
500-2000 person European manufacturers in metals, machinery, or food processing with a dedicated procurement function.
Understanding
Finance (Finance Director, CFO, or equivalent) holds the budget for spend management software at this company size. Software purchases above a threshold (possibly EUR 10,000-20,000/year [illustrative]) require Finance sign-off.
Learning
Ask in every procurement interview: "Who signed off the last software purchase your team made? Walk me through the process." In Finance Director interviews: "What software does your procurement team use? Who picked it and who paid for it?"
Limit
If 7 of 10 procurement leads confirm Finance signed off their most recent software purchase, H2 is validated and the GTM motion needs a full rebuild around Finance Director buyers.
Kill if...
7+ of 10 procurement leads confirm independent signing authority for software tools in the EUR 15,000-60,000 range [illustrative]. Finance involvement is limited to annual budget allocation.
Validate if...
7+ of 10 procurement leads describe a Finance Director or CFO as the final signatory on their most recent software purchase of this size.
H3 Buyers commit to annual contracts on first purchase, not monthly [illustrative] Assumption
Population
Heads of Procurement and Finance Directors at 500-2000 person European manufacturers evaluating or having recently purchased operational software.
Understanding
Buyers commit to annual contracts on first purchase because procurement software is operationally embedded and the evaluation cycle is long enough that a monthly opt-out is not a meaningful risk reduction.
Learning
Ask about the last software purchase: "Was it annual or monthly? Who decided the contract structure? What would it have taken to commit annual on day one?" Ask about what they agreed to, not what they would prefer.
Limit
If 6 of 10 interviewees describe their most recent operational software purchase as annual from the start, H3 is directionally supported.
Kill if...
More than half describe demanding monthly contracts initially, with annual commitments only after 3-6 months of proven use.
Validate if...
6+ of 10 describe their most recent operational software purchase as an annual contract from the first signature.
H4 Procurement leaders actively avoid their ERP's procurement modules because of complexity Assumption
Population
Heads of Procurement at 500-2000 person manufacturers currently running SAP, Oracle, or Microsoft Dynamics with procurement modules available but partially or fully unused.
Understanding
Procurement leads have access to ERP procurement modules but do not use them effectively because configuration is too complex, requires IT involvement for every change, and does not match how procurement actually works.
Learning
Ask: "Have you ever tried to use your ERP's procurement module? Walk me through it." Then: "Why did it stick or not stick?" Do not describe the problem. Let them describe whether it exists.
Limit
If 7 of 10 describe their ERP procurement module as partially or fully unused and attribute it to configuration complexity or workflow mismatch, H4 is directionally supported.
Kill if...
More than half describe their ERP procurement module as working well and used consistently. They see an additional tool as duplication.
Validate if...
7+ of 10 describe the ERP module as unused or inadequate, cite specific reasons, and have reverted to workarounds alongside the ERP.
H5 Manufacturers will share real spend data during the evaluation stage Assumption
Population
Procurement and IT teams at 500-2000 person European manufacturers evaluating new procurement software.
Understanding
Manufacturers will share category-level spend data during evaluation (pre-signature) to allow a meaningful proof of concept. Data governance concerns are manageable through anonymisation agreements and vendor security documentation.
Learning
Ask: "If you were evaluating a new procurement tool, what data would you be comfortable sharing before signing a contract? What would you need to see from the vendor before you shared it?"
Limit
If 6 of 10 describe willingness to share anonymised category spend data under a basic NDA before signature, H5 is conditionally supported.
Kill if...
More than half describe spend data as too sensitive to share with an unknown vendor before contract signing, regardless of security documentation.
Validate if...
6+ of 10 describe willingness to share category-level spend data under an NDA in the evaluation stage, provided the vendor has credible security documentation.
Exercise 2

Interview Plan

28-question script covering five themes: Intro, Industry, Business, Personal, and Outro. Every question tagged by hypothesis and Five Signal. A Finance Director variant covers H2 from the buyer side. 30-45 minutes per interview.

Consent Script: Use verbatim before any substantive questions
"Thanks for taking the time to participate in our study. Just so you know, the information shared will only be used for internal research purposes. With that, do you mind if I record this session?"
# Theme Question Tags Note
1 Intro "What's your job title and how long have you been in the role?" context Establishes seniority and tenure.
2 Intro "What was your role before this one?" context Surfaces domain depth and prior tool exposure.
3 Intro "How big is your procurement team, and how is it structured?" H2concrete behaviour Reveals whether a CPO exists above them and whether procurement has its own budget.
4 Intro "Walk me through what a typical Monday morning looks like for you." H1time and money spent Open question. Let them volunteer what takes their attention before directing the conversation.
5 Industry "What's happening in procurement in your sector right now? Where are you hearing about it?" signal: awareness level Surfaces market maturity and where procurement leads get information.
6 Industry "How has procurement complexity changed over the last two years in your vertical?" signal: recency and frequency Reveals whether the problem is getting worse. Post-merger manufacturers often flag a step-change.
7 Industry "What's the biggest challenge facing procurement teams your size right now?" signal: problem-area match Do not steer. Let them name the problem category. Unprompted admin burden is a strong signal.
8 Business "When did you last onboard a new supplier? Walk me through the first week." H1concrete behaviour Most important behavioural question. Push for specifics: "Who sent the first email? Who set up payment terms?"
9 Business "What's the last invoice dispute your team had with a supplier? Walk me through how it got resolved." H1concrete behaviour Invoice reconciliation is one of the two core time drains. A real dispute story surfaces the process and tools used.
10 Business "Who signed off the last software purchase your team made? What was the process?" H2concrete behaviour The H2 question. Ask it plainly. Follow up: "Is there a threshold below which you can sign off independently?"
11 Business "What's the biggest software tool your team has bought in the last 18 months? Walk me through that evaluation." H2H3recency and frequency Surfaces the full procurement journey. Annual or monthly contract tells you about H3 directly.
12 Business "Have you ever tried to use your ERP's procurement module? Walk me through it." H4concrete behaviour Do not describe the problem. Let them describe whether it exists. "Yes, it works fine" directly challenges H4.
13 Business "Why did that stick or not stick with the team?" H4concrete behaviour Follow-on from Q12. Listen for the specific reason: complexity, IT dependency, workflow mismatch.
14 Business "Can you show me the last month's supplier spend? How is it categorised?" H5concrete behaviour Direct data-sharing probe. If they pull up a report without hesitation, H5 is lower risk than assumed.
15 Business "When did your company last acquire or merge with another business? What changed for procurement?" signal: recency and frequency The ICP trigger question. A recent merger with unreconciled supplier databases is a live, urgent version of the problem.
16 Business "How many active suppliers does your team manage? How many were there 18 months ago?" H1time and money spent Growth in supplier count without growth in headcount is a direct proxy for admin burden.
17 Business "What does your team do when a supplier invoice does not match the purchase order? Walk me through the last time that happened." H1concrete behaviour PO-invoice mismatch is one of the most time-intensive manual tasks. A real example reveals whether the process is ad hoc or systematised.
18 Business "What tools does your team currently use for supplier communication, PO management, and invoice approval? Take me through the stack." H4problem-area match Reveals the current tool landscape. A mix of ERP plus email plus spreadsheets is the expected baseline.
19 Business "What's the last process improvement project your procurement team actually completed? What made it stick?" H4concrete behaviour Surfaces what makes operational change succeed in this environment. Useful for understanding implementation tolerance.
20 Personal "What's taking up most of your team's time this quarter?" H1time and money spent If supplier admin comes up unprompted, that is a strong problem-area match signal.
21 Personal "How many invoices does your team reconcile per month? How long does that take roughly?" H1time and money spent Push for a rough number. "300 invoices a month, takes 2-3 days to clear the backlog" is a data point for the ROI case.
22 Personal "What part of supplier management is most manual for your team right now?" H1problem-area match Open invitation to name the pain in their own words. The answer shapes the product language.
23 Personal "Have you had a supplier crisis in the last 12 months? Walk me through it." signal: recency and frequency Connects admin burden to commercial impact: cost overruns, production delays, board escalations.
24 Personal "What data or metrics does your team track about procurement performance? What do you share upward?" H1concrete behaviour Reveals whether procurement time is already measured. If they track cycle times, ask what the numbers are.
25 Personal "If you could remove one thing from your week, what would it be?" signal: awareness level Ask after 25-30 minutes of conversation. The answer at this point is less filtered than anything earlier.
26 Outro "If you had a magic wand for your procurement team on Monday morning, what one thing would you fix?" signal: awareness level Classic magic wand close. Ask it last. Often the most specific and honest answer in the whole interview.
27 Outro "Is there anyone else in your network I should be talking to? Someone who handles procurement differently, or at a different size manufacturer?" signal: referral Ask for a specific name, not a vague yes. Manufacturing networks are tight. Referrals carry real weight.
28 Outro "What's the one question I should have asked you that I didn't?" signal: awareness level Closing meta-question. Surfaces blind spots. Update the script if the same gap appears twice.

Interview Cadence: 5 Per Week, 20 Total

Manufacturing procurement leads are harder to reach than fintech buyers. Build the second-touch follow-up plan into every outreach attempt from the start.

Week Target Activity
Week 1 5 interviews Run interviews 1-5. Mix procurement leads and finance leads. Update script after interview 1. Focus on H1 and H2.
Week 2 5 interviews Run interviews 6-10. Note emerging patterns. Adjust Finance Director variant if needed.
After interview 10 Affinity mapping (3 hours) Cluster insights by theme. If H2 is validated, decide to rebuild GTM before running interviews 11-20.
Week 3 5 interviews Run interviews 11-15 with adjusted script and possibly adjusted buyer title.
Week 4 5 interviews Run interviews 16-20. Begin synthesis for Week 4 workshop.
Second-Touch Follow-Up Plan for Unresponsive Contacts

Day 1: first message referencing the specific acquisition or merger trigger. Day 7: second message from a different angle (reference an industry challenge, not the same trigger). Day 14: move on if no response after two messages. Do not send a third message. Manufacturing buyers have long memories and will remember persistent outreach.
