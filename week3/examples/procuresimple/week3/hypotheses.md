## Current Customer and Problem Statement

ProcureSimple believes that Heads of Procurement (and equivalent titles: Procurement Director, Chief Procurement Officer, Procurement Manager) at 500-2000 person European manufacturers in metals, machinery, or food processing spend significant time chasing suppliers and reconciling invoices each week, and that this administrative burden is largely manual. The team's Week 2 positioning evolved from a Czech SME beachhead (50-200 person manufacturers) toward a broader mid-market segment where procurement roles are more defined, spend is larger, and the decision to buy software is clearer. The team believes buyers in this evolved segment would pay for spend management software that automates the admin layer, provided the integration into their existing ERP is low-friction and the onboarding does not require months of IT involvement.

This positioning shift raises a critical new question: in the 500-2000 person segment, does Procurement own the budget for spend management tools, or does Finance? The answer changes the entire GTM motion.

## Top 5 Assumptions Ranked by Risk

1. **H1: Heads of Procurement at 500-2000 person European manufacturers spend 30%+ of their time chasing suppliers and reconciling invoices [illustrative].** This drives the entire ROI story. If the real number is 10-15%, the product is a nice-to-have, not a must-have, and the sales motion becomes much harder. This is the most load-bearing number in the business case.

2. **H2: Finance, not Procurement, is the budget holder for spend management software at this company size.** If Finance owns the budget, procurement-first outreach means ProcureSimple is pitching to the champion (who cannot sign) while ignoring the buyer (who can). The entire GTM motion changes: messaging, outreach channel, proof points, and the demo itself need to be rebuilt around a Finance Director audience. This is the single highest-risk assumption in the model.

3. **H3: Buyers commit to annual contracts on first purchase, not monthly [illustrative].** The financial model depends on annual ARR. If buyers in this segment expect monthly contracts with cancellation rights (which mid-market B2B buyers often do for new tools), the cash flow model breaks and sales cycle assumptions need revisiting.

4. **H4: Procurement leaders at this segment actively avoid their ERP's procurement modules because of complexity.** This is the displacement story. If ERP modules are actually in use and functioning adequately, ProcureSimple is not a replacement but an addition, which is a much harder sell with a different ROI case. The replacement story is only true if the ERP module is genuinely unused or unsatisfying.

5. **H5: Manufacturers will share real spend data in the evaluation stage.** Onboarding requires data access. If buyers are unwilling to share category-level spend data before signing a contract, the proof-of-concept model breaks. This was flagged as a hesitation in Week 2 interviews at smaller companies and is likely to be a stronger concern at 500-2000 person manufacturers with more formal data governance.

## PULL Hypotheses

### H1: Procurement admin time

**Population:** Heads of Procurement (or Procurement Director, CPO, Procurement Manager) at 500-2000 person European manufacturers in metals, machinery, or food processing who manage at least 50 active supplier relationships.

**Understanding:** We believe these buyers spend 30%+ of their working time [illustrative] on supplier chasing and invoice reconciliation tasks that are primarily manual: following up on missing invoices, correcting PO mismatches, chasing delivery confirmations, and cross-referencing spend across systems.

**Learning:** Ask interviewees to walk through their last week at work in detail. How many hours went to supplier-related admin? What does Monday morning look like? How many invoices does the team reconcile per month, and how long does each one take? Do not ask "is this a big problem." Ask them to walk through the time.

**Limit:** If 7 of 10 interviewees describe supplier admin as consuming more than 20% of their team's time and cite it as a genuine constraint on higher-value work, H1 is directionally validated. If the dominant answer is "we have this mostly automated" or "it takes maybe an hour a week," the ROI story needs rebuilding from a different pain.

**Kill criteria:** Fewer than 3 of 10 interviewees describe supplier admin as a meaningful time drain. Most describe existing tools or processes as adequate.

**Validate criteria:** 7 or more of 10 interviewees describe supplier admin as consuming significant time, express frustration with the manual nature of the work, and connect it to missed work of higher strategic value.

**Evidence rating:** Assumption. Source: Desk research on procurement team workloads and Week 2 interviews with smaller companies (different segment). No interviews with 500-2000 person manufacturer procurement leads yet.

---

### H2: Budget ownership

**Population:** 500-2000 person European manufacturers in metals, machinery, or food processing with a dedicated procurement function.

**Understanding:** We believe Finance (the Finance Director, CFO, or equivalent) rather than Procurement holds the budget for spend management software at this company size. At 500-2000 employees, the procurement team typically has headcount budget but not software budget. Software purchases above a certain threshold (possibly EUR 10,000-20,000/year [illustrative]) require Finance sign-off.

**Learning:** In every interview with a procurement lead, ask: "Who signed off the last software purchase your team made? Walk me through the process." In every interview with a Finance Director at a manufacturer, ask: "What software does your procurement team use? Who picked it and who paid for it?" The two perspectives together reveal the real buying structure.

**Limit:** If 7 of 10 procurement interviewees confirm that Finance signed off their most recent software purchase over a material threshold, H2 is validated and the GTM motion needs adjustment. If procurement leads consistently have independent signing authority up to EUR 30,000+ per year, H2 is wrong and the current procurement-first approach is correct.

**Kill criteria:** More than 7 of 10 procurement leads confirm they have independent signing authority for software tools in the EUR 15,000-60,000 range [illustrative]. Finance involvement is limited to annual budget allocation, not individual purchase decisions.

**Validate criteria:** 7 or more of 10 procurement leads describe a Finance Director or CFO as the final signatory on their most recent software purchase of this size.

**Evidence rating:** Assumption. Source: Week 2 discovery at smaller companies (50-200 employees) found that the Finance Director or owner approved software. Whether this pattern holds in the 500-2000 employee segment is untested.

---

### H3: Annual contract commitment

**Population:** Heads of Procurement and Finance Directors at 500-2000 person European manufacturers who are evaluating or have recently purchased operational software.

**Understanding:** We believe buyers at this segment commit to annual contracts on first purchase rather than month-to-month, because procurement software is operationally embedded and the evaluation cycle is long enough that a monthly opt-out is not a meaningful risk reduction for the buyer.

**Learning:** Ask about the last software purchase: "Was it annual or monthly? Who decided the contract structure? What would it have taken to get you to commit to annual rather than monthly on day one?" Do not ask "would you prefer annual." Ask what they actually agreed to and why.

**Limit:** If 6 of 10 interviewees describe their most recent operational software purchase as annual from the start, H3 is directionally supported. If most describe starting monthly and only committing to annual after 3-6 months of use, the financial model needs to account for a longer path to ARR.

**Kill criteria:** More than half of interviewees describe demanding monthly contracts initially, with annual commitments only after a proven track record of 6 months or more.

**Validate criteria:** 6 or more of 10 interviewees describe their most recent operational software purchase as an annual contract from the first signature.

**Evidence rating:** Assumption. Source: None. No pricing or contract structure research done with this segment.

---

### H4: ERP module avoidance

**Population:** Heads of Procurement at 500-2000 person European manufacturers currently running SAP, Oracle, or Microsoft Dynamics with procurement modules available but partially or fully unused.

**Understanding:** We believe procurement leads at this segment have access to ERP procurement modules but do not use them effectively because the configuration is too complex, requires IT involvement for every change, and was never set up to reflect how the procurement team actually works. As a result, the team has reverted to spreadsheets, email, and informal processes alongside the ERP.

**Learning:** Ask: "Have you ever tried to use your ERP's procurement module? Walk me through it." Then: "Why did it stick or not stick with the team?" Do not describe the problem. Let them describe whether it exists. If they volunteer that the module works fine, probe: "What does your team do when a supplier is not in the system? What happens when an invoice does not match the PO?"

**Limit:** If 7 of 10 interviewees describe their ERP procurement module as partially or fully unused and attribute it to configuration complexity or poor fit with their actual workflows, H4 is directionally supported. If most describe the module as working adequately, the replacement story breaks and ProcureSimple is positioned as an addition, not a replacement.

**Kill criteria:** More than half of interviewees describe their ERP procurement module as working well and being used consistently by the team. They would see an additional tool as duplication.

**Validate criteria:** 7 or more of 10 interviewees describe the ERP procurement module as unused or inadequate, cite specific reasons (configuration complexity, workflow mismatch, IT dependency), and have reverted to workarounds alongside the ERP.

**Evidence rating:** Assumption. Source: Week 2 competitive analysis noted ERP modules as a key alternative. No interviews with procurement leads at this company size about their actual ERP usage.

---

### H5: Spend data sharing

**Population:** Procurement and IT teams at 500-2000 person European manufacturers who are evaluating new procurement software.

**Understanding:** We believe manufacturers will share category-level spend data during the evaluation stage (pre-signature) to allow a meaningful proof of concept. We believe data governance concerns are manageable through anonymisation agreements and vendor security documentation, and that the desire to demonstrate ROI is strong enough to overcome initial hesitation.

**Learning:** Ask: "If you were evaluating a new procurement tool, what data would you be comfortable sharing before you signed a contract? What would you need to see from the vendor before you shared it?" Do not assume the answer is yes or no. Ask what the conditions would be.

**Limit:** If 6 of 10 interviewees describe a willingness to share anonymised category spend data under a basic NDA before signature, H5 is conditionally supported. If most describe requiring a signed contract before sharing any spend data, the POC model needs to work without real data in the first stage.

**Kill criteria:** More than half of interviewees describe spending data as too sensitive to share with an unknown vendor before contract signing, regardless of security documentation.

**Validate criteria:** 6 or more of 10 interviewees describe being willing to share category-level spend data under an NDA in the evaluation stage, provided the vendor has credible security documentation.

**Evidence rating:** Assumption. Source: Week 2 interviews found hesitation about data sharing at smaller companies. This concern is likely to be stronger at larger manufacturers with more formal data governance.

## Single Biggest Risk

**H2 is the single biggest risk.** If Finance owns the budget for spend management software at this company size, the current procurement-first GTM motion is targeting the champion instead of the buyer. Every message, demo, and outreach designed for a Procurement Head is landing with someone who cannot sign the contract. The fix is not minor: it requires rebuilding the sales motion, the outreach list, the proof points, and the messaging hierarchy. H2 must be tested in the first five interviews before any further GTM investment is made.
