## Outreach Templates

All templates are illustrative starting points [illustrative]. Personalise every message before sending. Generic messages produce generic response rates. Manufacturing buyers respond to specificity.

### Personalised cold email (acquisition/merger trigger)

Subject: Procurement at [Company name] after the [acquisition/merger]: quick question

> Hi [First name],
>
> I noticed [Company name] completed the [acquired company] acquisition [timeframe] ago. That kind of integration usually means your team is managing a significantly larger supplier base than it was 18 months ago.
>
> I am researching how heads of procurement at mid-market manufacturers handle supplier consolidation and spend visibility after a merger, specifically whether the manual admin burden creates a real constraint or whether most teams have found workable solutions.
>
> Would you be willing to spend 20 minutes sharing how your team is handling this? No pitch, just research. Happy to share what I am hearing from others in similar situations.
>
> [Your name]
> ProcureSimple

**Length:** 4 sentences maximum. Longer emails get lower response rates.

---

### LinkedIn opener (2 sentences)

> Hi [First name], I came across your profile while researching how procurement leads at European manufacturers handle supplier management after an acquisition. I am doing 20-minute research conversations this month. Would you be open to a brief chat?

**Note:** Do not mention the product. Do not say "I want to show you something." Ask only for a research conversation.

### Cold call script

**Opening:**
"Hi, could I speak to [Name]? My name is [Your name] from ProcureSimple."

**Reason for call:**
"I am doing research into how procurement teams at mid-market manufacturers handle supplier admin and spend visibility, particularly at companies that have gone through a merger or acquisition recently. I want to understand whether the manual workload is a real constraint or whether most teams have found ways to manage it. I am not pitching anything."

**Ask:**
"Would you be willing to spend 20 minutes on a call this week? I would be happy to share what I am hearing from procurement leads at similar manufacturers."

**If they say they are busy:**
"Completely understood. Could I send you a one-line email to follow up? I will be brief."

### Referral ask (after a good interview)

Use at the end of every interview where the conversation was substantive.

> "This has been really useful, thank you. I am doing 20 of these conversations across European manufacturers. Is there anyone in your network who handles procurement at a similar size manufacturer that you think I should speak to? Even one name would be genuinely valuable."

**Note:** Ask for a specific name, not a general yes. "Who is the first person that comes to mind?" Let them name someone rather than prompting with options. Manufacturing networks are tight and referrals carry real weight.

## Channel Mix Recommendation

| Channel | Proportion | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn cold outreach | 35% | Highest volume, lower response rate than in fintech. Personalise every message with the specific acquisition or merger trigger. |
| Trade shows (Hannover Messe, Anuga) [illustrative] | 30% | Face-to-face at trade events is the most effective channel for manufacturing buyers. Approach before the event if the exhibitor list is public. Follow up within 48 hours. |
| Warm intros (Hub201 advisor and mentor network) | 25% | Manufacturing is relationship-driven. Warm intros will outperform cold outreach significantly. Prioritise every warm intro before sending cold messages. |
| Industry bodies and working groups | 10% | Slower to yield meetings but builds long-term credibility. Being in the room at a VdMA or swissmem working group makes subsequent cold outreach land better. |

**Why warm intros matter more in manufacturing than in fintech:** Manufacturing procurement leads are generally harder to reach cold than fintech security leads. They have longer decision cycles, are more cautious about unknown vendors, and tend to buy on relationships. The Hub201 advisor with Serbian auto-parts manufacturing experience is a significant asset here. Every warm intro from this network is worth at least ten cold messages.

Adjust channel proportions after Week 2 based on actual response rates. If trade shows are producing better interview quality than LinkedIn, shift time and effort toward event attendance and pre-event outreach.

## Second-Touch Follow-Up Plan

Manufacturing buyers do not always respond to a first message. This is not a rejection. Follow this protocol before moving on.

| Step | Timing | Message |
|---|---|---|
| First touch | Day 1 | Personalised opener referencing the specific acquisition or trigger |
| Second touch | Day 7 | Different angle: "I have been hearing from procurement leads across metals manufacturers that post-merger supplier consolidation is creating real complexity this year. Is that something your team is navigating?" |
| Move on | Day 14 | If no response after two messages, move to "revisit in 60 days" column. Do not send a third message. Do not apologise. Do not chase. |

**Why a second touch works:** The first message establishes context. The second message comes from a slightly different angle, often referencing a different pain or a different trigger. In manufacturing, buyers are busy and may not have responded to the first message because the timing was bad, not because the topic is irrelevant.

**Why to stop at two touches:** Manufacturing buyers have long memories. A third message to someone who has twice not responded shifts the relationship from "interesting researcher" to "persistent vendor." That is hard to recover from.
